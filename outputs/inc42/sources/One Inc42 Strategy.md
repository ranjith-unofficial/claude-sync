## **00\. The vision on a page**

> ### **One Inc42: the intelligence engine for India's startup economy.**

> One product, one identity, one membership – content, data, community and AI together. Free for everyone building. Known by name at the core. Convened in rooms admission cannot buy.

In five lines:

1. Reach everyone building India's startup economy – free, open, amplified by social and video.  
2. Know the core by name – one registration, one identity, every surface.  
3. Deepen the core into members – content, data, community and AI in one membership that funds the product itself.  
4. Convene the best of them – in rooms that are earned, never bought.  
5. The market pays for the room – and the room pays for the engine.

**The engine:** millions reached → tens of thousands known → thousands of members → hundreds convened → the market buys the room's composition and funds the whole engine. Each stage feeds the next; the last stage pays for the first. The rooms produce our most credible stories, and social and video turn them back into reach – *room → film → reach → nominations → room.* An AI-native operation runs the deepening as automation, which is what makes *free for the millions, deeper for the core* economical.

**What we are – and are not:** *Curated, not exhaustive · Earned, not sold · Membership, not paywall.*

The full vision – the world we are building, the member's story, why now, and what stands still – is VISION.md. Everything below is how we get there.

## **1\. The problem, at the level that matters**

> Inc42 runs three businesses selling three different things to what is one audience, and no product, no metric and no owner spans them. The audience meets three companies. We run three P\&Ls. Everything in between is nobody's job.

Editorial monetises attention. Datalabs monetises access. Events and IPs monetise the room. Three revenue models → three roadmaps → three definitions of success – so cross-surface work has no owner, no metric, no budget line.

This is structural, not an execution failure, and it explains the evidence better than any story about design or effort: cross-surface usage sits at 1.6–3.4% (which is what three P\&Ls predict) · a large catalogue of product is built but never deployed because shipping across surfaces requires a shared definition of success that never existed · the former north-star metric was blind to 99.71% of arrivals · four unowned blockers gate ten roadmap items · and the external scoreboard says it plainest: we lose to entrackr on every engagement metric despite \~25% more raw traffic – traffic was never the constraint.

The operating assumptions this vision is built on – adopted, not hedged:

- H1 – one ecosystem, one ICP. Editorial and Datalabs serve overlapping audiences: the same startup ecosystem, on both sides. The low cross-surface numbers are a distribution failure, not two audiences.  
- H2 – the surfaces should combine around that ICP. One experience, drawing on everything already built, with the app as a natural *representation* of it – not the product itself.  
- H3 – the vision is benchmarked on a longer horizon than the evidence covers. Assumptions are required, not avoided – which is why this document carries a hypothesis ledger instead of a claim of proof.

Stated positively:

> We hold the ecosystem's attention, the ecosystem's record, and the ecosystem's room – and no single relationship with anyone standing in it. The vision is that single relationship.

## **2\. What Inc42 becomes**

> ## **One Inc42: the intelligence engine for India's startup economy.**

> One product, one identity, one membership – content, data, community and AI together – that identifies, activates and retains the ecosystem's core professionals. Free for the wider ecosystem. Deeper for the core. And the engaged, qualified audience it creates is the asset every Inc42 revenue line runs on.

The mechanics of the model, in one chain:

- Editorial and the free layer build reach and feed the top – for the millions, free forever.  
- One membership – not a content subscription, not a data subscription, but a unified offering of content \+ data \+ community benefits \+ the AI layer – engages and retains the core TAM. Its revenue is secondary by design; its output is a qualified, engaged, identified audience.  
- The rooms – summits, IPs, curated convenings – remain the primary business. They are already sold on the composition of who attends, guaranteed today through application curation and editorial invitation. The product's job is to feed and enlarge the qualified pool that curation selects from – and to make the composition of any room *measurable and warrantable* after the fact. And the rooms deepen engagement before they earn revenue: convening is where relationships deepen, and the community that runs between rooms is retention infrastructure the product keeps warm – the third pillar's own canon verb is CONVENE, not monetise.

**And for the member – the claim, in one line:** *one identity through which you track your market, look up any company, ask the corpus, meet your peers, and become visible to the rooms where the ecosystem meets – things that today take five disconnected products, none of which know each other.*

**What this is not:** a data-completeness contest with the incumbents (a race the category leader runs at negative EBITDA, and which we declined to fund), a content paywall (a category with a hard ₹2,500–7,500 ceiling that we exited once already), or a two-audience platform (a shape with no working precedent, named by one failed comparable's post-mortem as its cause of death).

## **4\. The operating model – decided, not open**

Subscriptions and membership will never be the primary revenue channel. They are secondary, and their primary purpose is engagement and retention. The rule that enforces it: Membership revenue is welcome to grow – but it is never bought at the audience's expense: no feature ships to make the number, no engagement mechanism is degraded to monetise, and the line's first duty is sustaining the product organisation that builds it. Beyond that, growth is a free win – not a target anyone is chased on.

That single decision resolves the bind that blocked this work for weeks – *the room monetises best but produces the least reading; data produces reading but sits in a small market whose leader loses money; news has a hard WTP ceiling and converts readers at 1.41%* – because reading is never asked to monetise directly. Reading builds and qualifies the audience; the audience powers the rooms; membership makes the audience identified, engaged and retained.

The consequences, to be read as rules:

1. Ancillary audiences stay free forever. Reach, SEO and brand for the millions, at near-zero marginal cost. Their non-conversion is not a failure; they were never a target.  
2. Editorial's job is to build and keep the audience – not to convert it. The 1.41% reader→payer rate is the category norm, not underperformance: no publisher anywhere bridges it, and the largest Asian comparable holds 36.8M followers and zero individual paying subscribers.  
3. Price maximises the number of qualified people in a paid relationship – not revenue per head. At equal revenue, the lower price buys a larger asset, and the asset is what the primary line sells.  
4. Composition is already what sponsors buy – promised in every IP, priced into the highest ticket sizes in the industry, guaranteed at the door by curation and editorial invites. The gap is not the business model; it is measuring and warranting it afterwards.

## **5\. The north-star metric**

> ### **Qualified Identified Actives (QIA)**

> People with a known role, inside the core professional market, who took a qualifying action in a rolling 30-day window. *(Window revised 2026-08-12, from 90 days – 30 matches the monthly operating cadence and is honest about recency; QIA-90 survives as a reach band. ⚠ The baseline below is the 90-day figure; the 30-day baseline must be pulled before targets are re-set.)*

Qualifying actions – any ONE, attributable to the identified person: a content read with measured depth (Scroll Depth restored first) · a profile, search or database view · a watchlist, saved-search or alert interaction · an Ask query · an app session · a newsletter click, never an open (auto-opens inflate) · an event application or registration · a community contribution · a payment or renewal. Never counted: email opens · push receipts · a bare login with no content action · staff and internal accounts (127 internal accounts once contaminated 21% of identified pageviews) · bots.

vs WEIU, the retired weekly north star – the honest comparison:

|  | WEIU | QIA-30 |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Window | Weekly – twitchy, seasonal | Rolling 30 days – stable, monthly cadence |
| Who counts | Any identified user, students and staff included | Qualified – known role, inside the core market |
| "Engaged" | Undefined | A named action list, with exclusions |
| Blindness to the anonymous | Blind to 99.71% of arrivals, no strategy to change it | Same blindness, by design – but paired with the gating strategy that grows the base, and with R30 (unique browsers reading 2+ pieces in 30 days) so the anonymous majority stays visible |
| Role in the org | The only lens – eight programmes optimised 0.29% of the business | One of two lenses: QIA for the relationship, R30 for the reach |

*Identified* excludes the 98% of sessions that are logged out · *qualified* excludes the aspirant volume · *active* excludes the 92% of named contacts who are dormant.

**The honest starting position, measured 2026-08-11:** 21,298 ninety-day identified actives · 43.2% classifiable to a role · 9.99% with role *and* resolved company – the real "qualified."

⚠ There is no third-party enrichment layer. A full warehouse scan for every major vendor returned nothing; every job title we hold is self-reported on a form. Coverage is form-gated (77.6% among recent payers, 21.6% across the base, 25.0% among app users), and joining our own Datalabs people database on LinkedIn slug yields zero new classifications – the populations don't overlap.

★ That is why registration is the central mechanism, not a formality: it is the only way we will ever learn who anyone is. Profiles and the database become free but registration-gated (articles stay open, no wall), and every capture point asks role \+ employer type, with a date stamp – a title alone cannot identify a service provider, and today no timestamp exists so no title's freshness is knowable. Gating adds identity, never price: nothing that is free today becomes paid, and the current free entitlements are unchanged.

The targets (FY convention · horizon FY31):

|  | FY28 | FY30 | FY31 (horizon) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| QIA-30 – the North Star (quality) | 15,000 | 35–40,000 | \~50,000 |
| IA-30 – the volume line (any identified person, qualifying action) | \~30,000 | \~80,000 | \~120,000 |

Computed from the driver model – gated-surface exposure × registration (A2, the dominant unknown) × role+employer completion × core-market share × 30-day activity – with enrichment shipping in FY28 in the base case. Final calibration on the 30-day baseline pull. Starting position: \~2,100 truly qualified today. *(All timelines use the Apr–Mar financial-year convention.)*

The denominators – TAM reconciled bottom-up × top-down (Aug 2026, tam-synthesis): the core market is \~70–180K professionals today, growing toward \~120–280K by FY31 – QIA's denominator (FY31's \~50K ≈ 28% penetration of the mid). The extended tier is \~150–500K – IA's denominator. The earlier 35–100K was the *funded-economy floor*, not the TAM: it excluded revenue-positive unfunded companies, the D2C economy and corporate tech leadership – segments our own events already sell to. Sensitivities in order: A2 gate conversion · enrichment ship date · the bootstrapped/D2C undercount.

## **7\. The product – one membership, one ladder**

One unified product line: content \+ data \+ community \+ AI in a single membership. Not a portfolio. Not two products for two audiences.

|  | Price | Who | Fence |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Free | ₹0 | Everyone | Identity – articles open; profiles and database registration-gated |
| T1 | ₹4,999–14,999/yr *(price point within the band, renewal treatment, and monthly-vs-annual: TBD – experimented or discovered)* | The core TAM | Utility: alerts, saved searches, capped exports, Deal Digest, reports, community, uncapped Ask |
| T2 | ₹25,000–50,000/yr | Power users of T1 – same audience, more depth | Volume meters as the upgrade trigger; hard capability as the wall: contact information, financials, cap tables, uncapped exports, analyst credits |
| T3 | *deferred – needs its own validation* | Invite-only | Standing; capacity-capped rooms. Ticketing is the funnel into it, not replaced by it |

Design rules, each earned from evidence:

1. T2 is not a second audience. No player anywhere charges practitioners a real subscription *and* charges a second audience more on the same ladder. The precedented shape is two depths for one audience – the buyer segment emerges from who upgrades; it is never marketed to.  
2. One ladder holds because both sides want the same object at different depths – the founder wants to be *in* the ecosystem's record; the investor wants to *query* it. The record is the infrastructure that makes one ladder possible. It is not the product's identity.  
3. ⚠⚠ Selection is never sold. T3 may offer visibility to a selection process and must never guarantee inclusion – the moment inclusion is purchasable, the credential is worthless and the IPs stop being an asset. Verification → invitation → invoice, in that order, always. The awards and lists stay at the gated-free end, never a paid entitlement.  
4. Rooms do not sit in T2. Comparables consistently migrated room access out of mid-tiers; rooms survive as a capacity-capped, invite-only top tier – which is T3's shape, later.  
5. Most of T1/T2 ships by re-fencing entitlements and giving surfaces to product already built – five report types and the Deal Digest are all built or prototyped with no user-facing surface. ⚠ Nothing from the built catalogue has ever shipped to a user (Deal Digest included – corrected 2026-08-11; it was a prototype). The work is shipping, not engineering. Alerts are the only genuinely new data capability.  
6. Griffin is completely separate. Not a tier, not a funnel, not a destination of this ladder. It appears in the evidence base only as proof that selection is priceable in India.

## **7a. The amplification layer – social, video and distribution**

Where they fit in the engine – and where they deliberately do not.

**Settled first:** owned social is not an identification line. It produced roughly 1% of identifications against tens of millions of reach – as an acquisition instrument the question is closed, and handing social a QIA target would create exactly the metric-without-rights scapegoat the ownership model warns against.

Its two real jobs:

1. Stage 0 of the engine – reach. Social and video are the free layer extended off-property: brand, top-of-funnel, and the widest expression of "free for everyone building." Measured on reach and brand, not on QIA.  
2. ★ The credential loop – making conferred status visible. Selection only functions as a credential if it is *seen*. The 30 Startups announcement, the summit film, the founder story: these are what make standing desirable, and the rooms are the most credible content source we own. The loop, named: room → video → reach → nominations → room. Every turn of it strengthens the credential thesis – selection worth putting on a deck – at near-zero marginal content cost.

Distribution – newsletters, search/Discover, syndication – is the reading path *into* the engine: it feeds stage 0 and, where a link lands on a gated profile, feeds identification directly (the Datalabs Weekly's \~40 entity deep-links are the model).

The social desk's own July finding says the same thing from below: output ran at 231% of plan while views ran at 64% – *"output was never the problem – distribution was."* The amplification layer's constraint is routing and craft (the desk's hook analysis found a 2.6× spread by hook construction alone), not production volume.

**One nuance the credential loop must respect:** the social plan deliberately excludes event clips from its production-count targets – cheap, abundant room footage must not satisfy goals meant for original IP series. So the loop uses room content as *reach and proof*, while the craft investment stays with the original series. Both are true; the targets keep them honest.

**And one seam that belongs to the engine room (§7b):** the desk found 126 of 248 multi-voice companies in a single month had no Inc42 post at all. The record already knows what the ecosystem is talking about; a coverage-gap pass from the corpus to the editorial and social queues is a ready automation with measured headroom.

**Boundary:** the social and video teams own their own plans, and the owners exist (video sits with its named owner, reporting into editorial leadership). This strategy fixes their *role in the engine* and the metrics that role carries – reach and credential visibility, never QIA. ⚠ One blocker to giving this layer a coherent metric: two video targets \~10× apart coexist in the planning stack (a quarterly aspiration vs the monthly plan) – unreconciled, and the layer cannot carry a role-metric until one number is agreed.

## **7b. The engine room – internal AI and the agent platform**

The unstated hypothesis in claim 4, now stated. "The membership sustains the product organisation" silently assumes that cost base stays roughly flat while QIA scales 4×. Nothing guaranteed that – until it is made true deliberately. That is the engine room's job, and it is now A11 in the ledger.

Internal AI adoption and transformation. The engine's manual seams are precisely its automatable ones: curation pools worked by hand · composition unmeasured · enrichment unbuilt (the capability exists in the data team; no layer is live) · the Datalabs Weekly hand-assembled · lifecycle messaging absent (terms lapse monthly with zero comms). The agenda – adopt AI across our own use cases for efficiency, and build internal tools, agents, systems and automations – is what makes "free for the millions, deeper for the core" economical rather than headcount-bound.

This work has a home, a method – and now a ratified architecture. The internal-AI programme runs as its own workspace (scaling \~3 → 20–30 users through a single ranked backlog, 1–3-week MVPs), and the agent platform locked its architecture on 2026-08-12 – fifteen locks, among them: an agent is a scope \+ a capability grant \+ skills \+ delivery rules; a broker on every call; a principal argument on every retrieval; two-sided provenance; routine-over-code; earned promotion with kill conditions. The engine's seams above enter through the backlog and its scoring – they do not bypass it.

And the engine room is not hypothetical – part of it already runs in production. The n8n editorial pipelines – News AI v2's writer→judge draft flow, Markets AI, and Inc42 Scout answering over 75K companies and 60K articles – are live in Slack today, with an eval discipline the platform is adopting rather than reinventing. The platform's own review found three deployment classes – conversational agents (the new worker pool), pipeline agents (n8n – excellent, stays), broadcast agents (crons) – under one catalog.

The symmetry, in the platform's own words: "the intelligence engine, turned inward." The company knows everything about the ecosystem except who's asking; internally, the team knows everything and cannot answer what-we-decided. The company brain is *know the core by name*, applied to knowledge – and the dual-use thesis makes it strategic: the query-tools-behind-endpoints that serve internal agents are the same ones that will serve the member-facing AI layer later (ask-inc42 is the existence proof). The internal platform is the low-stakes rehearsal for the AI layer the membership defers.

**The honest constraint stands:** one operator today. A11 is an adoption bet, and the platform's own Move 1 is the named adoption test – gated, with kill conditions, and still waiting on its ownership decision.

**Boundary:** the internal-AI and agent-platform desks own their roadmaps and their known blockers (ownership among them). This strategy fixes the *interface*: the seams above are the priority automation surface – plus the coverage-gap pass from §7a – and the metric is product-organisation cost per QIA, tracked from Horizon 1 – falling, or at minimum flat while QIA multiplies.

## **11a. The hard questions, answered first**

The best plans attack themselves before anyone else can. The four hardest objections, answered from the evidence base:

Q1 – Won't registration-gating the profiles kill the traffic that feeds them? Honestly unknown – which is why it ships behind a holdout (A2), with its measuring instrument restored first. What the evidence says: articles are never gated; the closest comparable meters entity profiles at every tier including free and thrives on search; and the gate is the *only* remaining path to knowing who anyone is, because there is no enrichment layer and every title we hold is self-reported.

Q2 – Why will a membership work when Plus and Pro both failed? Because they failed on category, not appetite. Plus metered *content* in a market with a hard news-WTP ceiling. Pro sold *standalone data access* and renewed 0 of 17 – while price cuts of 85% and 30% moved no volume, proving price was never the constraint. The membership is a different category – content \+ data \+ community \+ AI in one – with a working Indian precedent at the same price point, and its renewal is instrumented from day one (A9) instead of discovered later.

Q3 – Doesn't a T2 full of enablers cannibalise sponsorship? Different job: T2 sells *depth and query*; sponsorship sells *presence in a curated room and its composition*. The overlap is hypothesis A7 and gets measured, not assumed. The guards: contact information sits behind the T2 fence, and any member-to-member access is gated and curated – an ungated tier would turn T1 into a lead list and churn it.

Q4 – What if the legacy base simply doesn't migrate? Then we find out cheaply and early: the migration offer needs nothing built, and it carries a pre-registered bar – below \~20% on a representative sample, the prices are reworked before anything is built (A1). The probe explores the ₹4,999–14,999 band – whose floor sits at today's realised modal order, so entry carries near-zero effective price increase.
