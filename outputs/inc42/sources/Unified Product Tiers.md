## **1\. What this replaces, and why the failure does not predict the outcome**

Inc42 Plus was removed. It failed on category, not price – it metered *content*, and Indian news willingness-to-pay caps at ₹2,500–7,500/yr (Moneycontrol Super PRO lists ₹7,499 and sells at ₹2,499; ET Prime ₹2,599–6,499). No amount of packaging beats that ceiling while the product is "pay for articles." Utkarsh's own diagnosis – no focus, no clear value prop on the metering-content side – and the market data agree.

A unified membership at \~₹20K is therefore not a 3× price increase on Plus. It is a different category – one where GrowthX sells at ₹19,999/yr in India today. The Plus failure predicts nothing about it, and that is the single most important framing in this document.

Datalabs stays cheap deliberately. The enterprise/team tier (₹50,000/user/yr, min 5 seats) failed on GTM, and the honest strategic reason not to retry it now: Tracxn was cutting price to defend the same market. A margin race against a competitor already discounting is not a battle worth funding. So data becomes an entitlement that makes a membership worth renewing – not a product defending a price point. This also fits the strongest retention signal we hold: data purchasers are the payers who keep reading (75.9%, vs 8.3% for content subscribers).

## **2\. The ladder**

Revised 2026-08-11 (Utkarsh). Earlier prices in this document were too high and are superseded.

|  | Price | Who | Retention engine |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Free | ₹0 | The 5–15M ancillary audience | Not applicable – never a monetisation target |
| Tier 1 | ₹4,999–14,999/yr *(band set 2026-08-13; point, renewal treatment, monthly-vs-annual: TBD)* | The core TAM | Community \+ data utility |
| Tier 2 | ₹25,000–50,000/yr | Power users of T1 – same product, more depth | Depth \+ data dependency |
| Tier 3 | *deferred* | Invite-only, curated rooms | Standing |

### **★ Why the lower prices are right, and why my earlier ones were not**

This desk argued that under Option 2 price should be set to maximise the number of qualified people in a paid relationship, not revenue per head – and then recommended ₹20K, which does the opposite.

|  | Subs to reach ₹1.13 Cr | Qualified audience created |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| ₹20,000 | 566 | 566 |
| ₹7,500 | 1,500 | 1,500 |

Same revenue, 3× the asset – and the asset is what the primary revenue line sells.

There is a second argument. Current realised ARPU is ₹6,921 with a ₹4,128 modal order against a ₹4,999 list. At ₹5–10K, T1 is roughly today's price for a substantially better product. Migration stops being a risky repricing and becomes a value-add, which collapses the largest risk in the earlier version of this spec.

### **★★ T2 is a power user of T1 – not a second audience**

Decision (Utkarsh, 2026-08-11), and it corrects a real error in this desk's proposal. The desk had proposed splitting T1/T2 by *audience* – T1 for founders and operators (the supply side), T2 for investors, service providers and corporates (the demand side buying deal-flow tools).

⚠ That would recreate two product lines inside the fix for two product lines – the identical fragmentation this entire exercise exists to solve, one level down. One unified product line is the goal; an audience split defeats it.

And the already-gathered evidence supports the single ladder. Both verified bundled comparables do exactly this: DealStreetAsia is "one ladder, data depth as the upsell" and Tech in Asia meters entity access at every tier including free ($0 → $59 → $199 → $599). Neither targets a second audience. The professional buyer self-selects upward because they need depth.

> **HYPOTHESIS (to validate, not a set direction):** a single ladder fenced on depth serves both the audience side and the demand side without splitting into two products. The buyer segment emerges from who upgrades; it is not marketed to.

**How it gets validated:** measure who actually buys T2. If T2 fills with investors, service providers and corporates, the hypothesis holds *and* the demand side has self-identified for free. If T2 stays empty while those buyers ask for something else, the split was real and the ladder needs rethinking.

**Design consequence:** the fence between T1 and T2 must be sharp, not a volume gradient – otherwise a service provider pays ₹7,500 for what they would have paid ₹37,500 for. Contact information and uncapped exports are the natural fence.

### **Tier 3 – deferred**

Utkarsh, 2026-08-11: T3 needs its own brainstorming, research and validation, and is not being decided now. Recorded hunches: it is invite-only (which is also what keeps selection conferred rather than sold), it carries the curated-room entitlements, and – importantly – it does not replace ticketing. Ticketing becomes a funnel into T3. That is a better structure than the replacement model this desk proposed, because it keeps the low-commitment entry point that discovers who belongs in the room.

### **⚠⚠ The hard constraint on Tier 2 – you cannot sell selection**

Lane C is unambiguous, and Griffin's own SOP proves it in-house: verification → call → audited financials → invite → *then* invoice. You cannot pay your way in, and that is exactly why the credential is worth anything.

So Tier 2 may offer *visibility to the selection process* – a complete verified profile, presence in the consideration set, a route to being seen – and must never guarantee inclusion. The moment inclusion is purchasable, the credential is worthless and the IPs stop being an asset. This is the single easiest thing to get wrong in this spec.

## **5\. The FY28 feasibility proof – illustrative, not a prescription**

*This arithmetic demonstrates the line CAN sustain the product organisation at plausible volumes. It is not the plan – the plan derives from the engine's evidence.*

Baseline: ₹32.7 lakh collected in the last 12 months, run-rate ₹19.4 lakh, declining 33% year on year, across 1,045 billable-active subscriptions.

★ The unlock is that this is not net-new from zero. There are 1,045 active subscriptions to migrate, and 31.2% of Plus payers have already bought twice – repeat willingness exists in \~2,800 of 8,955 lifetime payers against a renewal mechanism that does not exist.

At the revised prices – T1 ₹7,500, T2 ₹37,500 – all three horizons clear:

| Horizon | T1 | T2 | Total | Target |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Year 1 | 800 × ₹7,500 \= ₹60L | 100 × ₹37,500 \= ₹37.5L | ≈₹1 Cr *(+T3 later)* | ₹1–2 Cr ✅ |
| Year 3 | 2,500 × ₹7,500 \= ₹1.9 Cr | 400 × ₹37,500 \= ₹1.5 Cr | ≈₹3.4 Cr *(+T3)* | ₹4–5 Cr ✅ with T3 |
| Eventually | 5,000 × ₹7,500 \= ₹3.75 Cr | 800 × ₹37,500 \= ₹3 Cr | ≈₹6.75 Cr *(+T3)* | ₹8–10 Cr ✅ with T3 |

★ The volumes are far more plausible than the earlier high-price version. 5,000 T1 subscribers at the endpoint is \~3–7% of the reconciled core market (\~70–180K); the ₹20K version required matching Tracxn's entire national user base to reach the same place. And year 1's 800 T1 sits against 1,045 already-active subscriptions – it is a migration and retention problem before it is an acquisition problem.

### **★ The one number everything turns on – and the test**

The migration rate of the legacy book. Make the offer to a sample of the 982 Plus and 63 Pro subscribers across the ₹4,999–14,999 band – 2–3 price cells, pooled \~20% bar (cells read directionally). The band's floor sits at today's realised modal order, so entry migration carries near-zero effective price increase. The probe answers the load-bearing assumption in weeks, earns revenue while doing so, and requires nothing to be built first. *(Corrected 2026-08-13: earlier versions of this probe were priced at the superseded ₹20K, then ₹9,999.)*

**Pre-registered bar:** if a representative sample migrates below \~20%, the base case is unreachable and the tier prices – not the product – need rework before anything is built.

**Also in year one, independent of the tiers:** \~₹30–50L from simply building a renewal motion (15% auto-renew today, with 26–142 Plus terms ending every month with zero communication), and ₹5–7L realistically recoverable from failed charges and recent on-hold subscriptions. *(The wider collection leak is not the lever it appeared to be – 88% of the ₹1.25 Cr on-hold book is more than two years past due.)*

## **6\. The design question that is not yet answered**

GrowthX retains on a curriculum and cohort bonds. We have neither. So what makes someone renew at T1's price in month twelve?

| Candidate | Evidence for | Evidence against |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| The data | Data buyers are the payers who keep reading – 75.9% | 0 of 17 Pro renewals. Data alone has never retained anyone here |
| The community | Cheapest to start; proven format in India | Hard to sustain; degrades quietly and late |
| Standing | The only mechanism Inc42 has demonstrated *at price* | Expensive to confer at 500-person scale |

Working answer: T1 retains on community \+ data utility; T2 retains on standing \+ full data. That is also the substantive reason they are two products rather than two prices.

⚠ This must be chosen deliberately and instrumented from day one. Plus died of an unclear value proposition; the failure mode here is identical and arrives twelve months after launch, when it is expensive to fix.

## **8\. Research trail – context, not decisions**

### **★★ Fence design – and the two research lanes disagree**

pricing-india-memberships.md and pricing-global-structures.md reached different answers about what separates tiers, and the disagreement is real rather than a measurement artefact. Both are recorded; neither is blended away.

|  | What fences tiers |
| :---- | :---- |
| India (TiE, GrowthX, IAN, IPV, SaaSBoomi) | Human time (the ₹6K→₹25K step is bought almost entirely with unlimited 1:1 mentoring replacing group mentoring) and status/rights at the top (governance vote, jury and speaking slots, deal flow). No Indian membership studied fences on seats, exports, API or content depth. |
| Global (Crunchbase, Wellfound, The Information, Sifted, Pavilion, Reforge) | Volume meters – Crunchbase 2K→5K export rows, Wellfound 20→250→1,000 contact reveals – *the only fence with a self-serve upgrade trigger*. Then proprietary data \+ query capability (The Information Pro $999: org charts, 19 databases, AI search; Sifted Pro £1,750: trackers, Leaderboards). And underrated: identity – Pavilion Associate $1,375 vs Executive $2,700 differ by \~2 features and a job title you cannot buy, at 10,000+ members. |

### **The resolution: global mechanics at Indian price points**

Inc42's asset is a database, not a mentor bench. Fencing on human time is the Griffin trap – 70–80% COGS, unscalable, and off-strategy for a media and data company. Use the global fence mechanics (volume meters, data depth, query capability, identity) and the Indian price bands. Inc42's scalable analogue of "human time" is analyst credits – which already exist as a Pro entitlement – not literal mentoring.

### **★★★ The finding that should discipline this plan**

> No player in either sample charges practitioners a real subscription *and* charges a second audience more on the same ladder. A ₹5–10K founder tier plus a ₹25–50K investor tier is unprecedented.

The two precedented shapes are:

1. A free identity-gated rung \+ one paid rung (Sifted, Tech in Asia, Wellfound), or  
2. Two paid rungs for the same audience at two depths.

★ Shape 2 is exactly what Utkarsh specified – T2 as a power user of T1, same audience. His correction moved this spec from the unprecedented structure to the precedented one. The desk's audience-split proposal was the unprecedented one.

⚠ And the warning is not hypothetical. On Deck's own post-mortem reads: *"serving multiple user groups and building flywheels between them… fractured our focus and brand."* That is this desk's earlier hypothesis stated as a cause of death. AngelList split into two companies at 8M users. Business of Fashion, mature and profitable, deliberately keeps Careers and Insights off the reader ladder.

### **★ The rule that reconciles one ladder with two audiences**

> One ladder works if and only if both audiences want the *same object* at different depths, with complementary verbs. *The founder wants to be in the entity graph. The investor wants to query it.*

Inc42's database passes this test. A services marketplace or a founder curriculum would not. So the ladder must be built on the database – that is what makes the single product line coherent rather than aspirational.

### **★★ The free rung should be identity-gated**

Every working one-ladder instance makes the practitioner rung free *and identity-gated*, and couples the sides: the free side supplies the data the paid side buys.

Inc42's free tier is free but not identity-gated – 98% of sessions are logged out. The fix is precisely Lane C's earlier finding: gate the entity, not the article.

- Articles: open, no wall. (Wave 0 removes the freewall; it costs first-visit depth and returns only 17.4% of identifications.)  
- Profiles and database: free, but registration-gated. ★ This is the qualified-audience engine – the thing sponsors ultimately pay for – and it costs no new revenue because nothing paid becomes free or vice versa.

★ Sifted's device is worth stealing outright: Pro includes *"automatic approval for your portfolio and accelerator companies to join Sifted for Startups."* The paying subscriber's benefit includes the right to distribute free memberships. For Inc42 that means a T2 investor or service provider can grant identity-gated free access to their portfolio – which grows the qualified audience as a paid benefit.

### **★ Rooms as a fence: they migrate out of mid-tiers, and survive only capacity-capped at the top**

**Two independent instances, same direction:** The Information's $749 tier bought "guaranteed access to all events" in 2017; the same price bucket now buys databases and AI search. Sifted Pro launched on Briefings \+ Roundtables; roundtables are gone from the 2026 page, replaced by data trackers, and Summit access is now merely a 25% discount.

But Business of Fashion still runs the room fence and charges 4.5× for it ($400 → $1,800), capacity-capped at four cities quarterly.

→ Rooms do not hold as a T2 fence. They hold as a capacity-capped, invite-only top tier – which is exactly the T3 shape. This supports deferring T3 as its own thing and warns against putting room access into T2.

### **★ Selection and price are independent axes**

Sifted's invite-only tier is free. Pavilion Gold's is paid but unpurchasable. So invite-only does not imply expensive, and paid does not imply purchasable.

→ Inc42's awards and lists (30 Startups, FAST42, UpNext, Minicorn) belong at the gated-free end, not as a paid entitlement. That protects the credential – consistent with the hard constraint in §3 – and it means the IP selection machinery works *for* the ladder without being sold on it.

### **Pricing shape – charge more in year one, less at renewal**

Four independent Indian organisations do this, inverse of SaaS practice: GrowthX −50% (₹19,999 → ₹9,999), TiE Charter −50%, IAN −29%, plus EO/YPO initiation models. Buyers expect it.

→ *(Superseded 2026-08-13 by the ₹4,999–14,999 band – kept as research context:)* T1 at ₹9,999 year one → ₹4,999 renewal matches GrowthX's exact shape, improves year-one revenue, and lands the renewal price at roughly today's realised modal order (₹4,128) – so legacy migration happens at no effective price increase at all, which is the cheapest possible answer to the migration risk.

### **Warnings carried**

1. ⚠ If T2 confers access to T1, it must be gated – an ungated T2 turns T1 into a lead list and churns it. Curated introductions, never a directory.  
2. ⚠ Leap.club died in May 2025 at ₹30,000/yr. T2's band is not free money.  
3. ⚠ Price T1 nearer ₹5,000 than ₹10,000 at renewal – SaaSBoomi gives the community layer away entirely free, so community alone cannot carry the tier.  
4. ⚠ No membership in either sample publishes a retention or renewal rate. The only figure anywhere is PitchBook's \~103% in a 10-K. We have no external benchmark for renewal and must instrument our own from day one.
