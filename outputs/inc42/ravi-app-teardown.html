His verdict — read this before anything below

Define the primary use case and the audience segments first. Everything else on this page is cosmetic until you do.

The trap he named: if you treat the wrong thing as the core use case, incremental changes on top of it will also produce bad data — because the core is right for you and wrong for the user. In the beginning, don't hesitate to scrap and rebuild. Run wild experiments, not tweaks. Ship what's already designed meanwhile, and collect data.

Brief

7 changes · the core

He used the screen and still could not say what it was for. Brief and Explore read as the same product to him.

Core
Merge Brief and Explore into one feed. Brief becomes “top headlines” inside it.
The two surfaces overlap, so neither earns its own tab. One feed, one mental model.
Core
Kill the horizontal scroll. Go vertical, Inshorts-style.
Jacob's Law — people arrive expecting a vertical feed and spend their first seconds fighting the gesture.
Core
Never re-serve a story the user has read. Open at story 6, not story 1.
Repetition creates fatigue, fatigue drops them to once a day, and once a day decays to zero.
High
Say, on the screen, that the brief is personalised.
Nowhere does the app claim it — so the work is invisible and the value never lands.
High
Make recency a weight in ranking, not a hard filter.
A hard cutoff means a user who missed two days can never resume; a weight lets them.
High
Personalise the order of stories. Question whether to personalise the selection at all.
At Inc42's daily publish volume there may not be enough stories for selection-level personalisation to mean anything.
Demote
Weekly recap and weekend brief must not hold homepage prominence.
Bells and whistles. They take space the core use case has not yet earned back.
Three independent sources, one finding. Ravi's “what is Brief?” matches the post-launch data (all brief loss sits at card 1, 45% advance) and the 20 Aug Brief Card UX session. This is not one person's taste.

Companies

2 changes

Company data is the deepest earned engagement in the app — and the screen opens as if nobody arrives with a name in mind.

High
Lead with search, not a discovery scroll.
People come to this tab looking for one company. Make that the first thing on the screen.
High
“Recently funded” must show amount raised and valuation — not the generic card.
The numbers are the differentiator. A generic card spends the slot and says nothing DataLabs alone could say.

1 change
High

Push notifications

1 bug · iOS

Not a design note — a root cause. This is the likely explanation for “push never fired” in the post-launch data.

Cause
The app sends a silent / remote push, which never delivers when the app is force-killed from the switcher.
iOS will not wake a force-quit app to decide what to render, so the notification simply never appears.
Fix
Send an APS alert payload — title, body, sound — with the ID in data.
FCM → APNs then renders it directly instead of waking the app to check the push type.
Test
Create an iPhone-user segment and read the delivery report.
A fresh install will not reproduce the failure, so testing on a clean device proves nothing.

Metrics he prescribed

Across the app
Measure What he said
App opens per day per user The single most important metric for an information app. Everything else is downstream of it.
Cohort & frequency D0 → D1 → D2 retention chart, plus RFM segments.
Benchmark ~3.5 opens/day/user, or ~30 min/user — measured on strangers, not testers or early adopters.
The red flag If early adopters aren't giving good numbers, it's very bad. They'll read anything.

The method he taught

For the IA rebuild

Card sorting — how to find the natural information architecture instead of arguing about it. His example: where does “diamond rings” belong on a jewellery site?

  1. List the use cases the app actually serves.
  2. Map each use case to an area of the app.
  3. Have 5–10 people group them, independently.
  4. The majority pattern is your natural IA. Build that.
