Opening

Unscored 4 min

Do not ask him to walk through his resume. You have read it; the time is better spent making him choose what matters, which is itself a signal.

#QuestionStrong answer sounds likeRed flagWhy ask it
O1 MUST “Of everything on your resume, which one piece of work would you want me to judge you on? Just that one.” Picks a shipped thing with a measured outcome — most likely the Figs KYC funnel or the n8n agent — and can say why it beats the rest. Picks the 18% AUM line, or can't choose and re-narrates the whole resume chronologically. Forces self-assessment. What he rates highest tells you what he thinks the job is.
O2 IF TIME “You applied to a media and intelligence company after two fintech internships. What made you apply here?” Has actually used Inc42 — names the app, DataLabs, a specific IP, or an article. Curiosity about the product, not the brand. Generic startup-ecosystem enthusiasm. Has not opened the product. Cheap test of whether he does homework — the same instinct the role needs daily.

#QuestionStrong answer sounds likeRed flagWhy ask it

Describe one of them to me.”Resume: Figs, goal-based investing, Jun–Nov 2025 A specific human — first-time retail investor, age, what confused them, where they abandoned. C3 IF TIME “Your quick-commerce study benchmarked four apps over 50+ queries. What made you start it — nobody asked you to.” Personal friction he noticed as a user, then systematised. Shows the instinct to go look without being assigned. It was a fellowship assignment presented as self-driven. This project is the closest thing on his resume to the work you actually do. Worth knowing if it was his idea.

Analytical judgement

25% · scored 75 10 min

Your bar is separating signal from noise, not producing reports on request. The reference point is the brief-ranking simulation: running the scorer over 366 real articles and finding 86% carried no sector tag, which killed the feature. You are listening for someone who would have found that.

#QuestionStrong answer sounds likeRed flagWhy ask it
A1 MUST “Take the KYC funnel. Before you changed anything — how did you work out where people were dropping, and how did you know that was the real problem and not a symptom?” Step-by-step: instrumented the steps, looked at the drop between them, segmented by device or document type, then checked the obvious explanation and ruled it out. Names what he expected to find and didn't. "We saw drop-off at KYC so we simplified KYC." A conclusion with no interrogation in between. The single highest-signal question in this bucket. Tests whether he reasons about causes or just reads a chart.
A2 MUST “Here's a real one from our app. Users pick sectors during onboarding to personalise their daily brief. The feature is live, and the ranking barely changes what anyone sees. Where would you look?” Asks about the data behind the tag before touching the algorithm — coverage, how sectors get assigned, who assigns them. Suspects an input problem, not a logic problem. Goes straight to tuning weights, adding ML, or "we should A/B test it." A live problem you already solved, so you know the answer. Anyone who asks about tag coverage is thinking the way the job needs.
A3 IF TIME “What's a number you reported that you later realised was misleading?” A real one, told without flinching, with what he changed afterwards. "I can't think of one." At nine months, everyone has one. Tests intellectual honesty — which matters more than usual given the projection-vs-result issue in his resume.

Execution & impact

25% · scored 73 10 min

Something shipped, and it moved a number he personally influenced. Team-level "contributed to" framing is weak evidence — and his resume has some.

#QuestionStrong answer sounds likeRed flagWhy ask it
E1 MUST “Walk me through the KYC rework from the day you proposed it to the day the 10% showed up. Who had to agree, what got cut, how long did it take?”Resume: “cutting verification drop-off by 10%” Names people and friction — an engineer who pushed back, a compliance constraint, a scope cut. Knows how long it took. The 10% arrives with a before-and-after and a measurement window. Frictionless story where everyone agreed. No timeline. The 10% has no baseline attached. Real shipping has scars. The absence of any is usually the absence of ownership.
E2 MUST “You shipped the MVP at Figs. What did you decide not to build, and what did that cost you?” A specific cut with a named trade-off he still stands behind or has since revised. "We built everything in the spec." Suggests he executed a list rather than owning scope. Deciding what not to build is the part of product sense that survives into a trainee role.
E3 IF TIME “All three of your roles were internships ending in under six months. What would change about how you work if you owned something for two years?” Recognises he has never lived with his own consequences, and says what he'd do differently. Insists nothing would change. Honest framing of his real gap — nine months, no full-time role — without making it an accusation.

His highest score, and the bucket where candidates inflate most. Your bar: built something shippable, or used AI inside the analysis itself. Tool names count for nothing. He has two real-looking builds — an n8n research agent and a RAG chatbot — so this is verification, not discovery.

#QuestionStrong answer sounds likeRed flagWhy ask it
AI1 MUST “Your n8n agent scrapes Reddit and Hacker News and drafts PRDs. Show me the workflow, or draw the nodes for me. Where does it break?”Resume: n8n AI Product Research Agent Describes actual nodes — trigger, fetch, dedupe, an LLM call, output. Volunteers failure modes: rate limits, hallucinated themes, junk from low-quality threads. Has opinions about the prompt. Stays at outcome level. Can't say what breaks. "It just works." You build n8n workflows daily and this hiring agent is one. You will know within ninety seconds whether he actually built it.
AI2 MUST “Your RAG bot grounds every answer in cited sources. What did you do about the cases where it retrieved the wrong fund?”Resume: RAG chatbot, 5 mutual funds, 30+ fields Talks about chunking, retrieval failures, evaluation — even crudely. Admits it got things wrong and says how he caught it. Claims it never retrieved wrongly. That answer is always false and reveals he never evaluated it. Directly relevant to AskInc42. Whether he tested his own AI output is the difference between a builder and a demo-maker.
AI3 MUST “Tell me about a time an AI tool gave you a confidently wrong answer and you caught it. How?” A concrete instance with the verification method he used. His own AI-trust research suggests he thinks about this. Generic "you should always verify AI output" with no example. The JD asks for someone who can "evaluate, debug and improve AI outputs." This is that line, made testable.

Written communication

20% · scored 72 4 min live + take-home

He claims PRD writing three times and names no artifact. His resume itself reads clean and specific, which is the primary evidence — but 20% of the decision should not rest on a one-page CV. Most of this bucket is better tested after the call than during it.

#QuestionStrong answer sounds likeRed flagWhy ask it
W1 MUST “Send me one PRD you actually wrote — the real file, not a rewrite. Which one will you send?” Names it immediately and can say who read it and what they did because of it. Offers to "put something together." Means no PRD exists in the form claimed. The claim appears three times on the resume. Either an artifact exists or the claim is decoration.
W2 MUST Take-home, one page, 48 hours: “Our newsletter deep links open in Safari instead of the app. Write the problem statement and how you'd find the cause.” Structured, states assumptions, proposes how to verify rather than jumping to a fix. Readable in one pass. Padded, hedged, or a generic troubleshooting listicle. Length used to substitute for clarity. A real open problem on your plate. You already know the answer is the click-tracker breaking Universal Links, so you can grade the reasoning honestly.
W3 IF TIME “Explain goal-based investing to me as if I've never invested.” Plain language, no jargon, one concrete example. Checks whether you followed. Reaches for finance vocabulary to sound credible. Verbal analogue of the writing test, and quick. The role turns analysis into recommendations other people act on.

Research ended at the slide. Insight never met a product. Tests whether research is a habit that changes things, or an artefact he produces.

Three claims to verify

Do not skip woven through

These are the screening agent's specific concerns. Each is a claim that may not survive contact — ask them inside the relevant bucket rather than as an interrogation block.

Projections dressed as results

“projected 25% CTR lift” · “projected 30% drop-off reduction”

The InvestMates lines are forecasts, formatted identically to the Figs 10%, which appears to be real. A reader skimming sees five measured wins where there may be one.

Ask: “Which of those numbers were measured after launch, and which were forecasts?” — the test is whether he separates them unprompted.

The 18% AUM claim

“drove 18% growth in AUM” — GTM intern, 3 months

A three-month intern claiming to have driven assets-under-management growth at a wealth firm. Possible, but attribution is doing heavy lifting.

Ask: “What was your specific contribution to the 18%, and what else was happening at the firm that quarter?”

User research with no n

“76% got wrong AI outputs, 84% wanted uncertainty signals”

Percentages with no sample size and no method. Could be 200 respondents or 12 friends.

Ask: “How many people, recruited how? What surprised you enough to change the design?”

Logistics 5 min
#QuestionWhat you're establishingWatch forContext
X1 MUST “You're on ₹1.8L and asking ₹6L. Talk me through that number.” A reasoned figure — market rate, cost of living, a competing offer. Flexible if the work is right. Anchoring hard at ₹6L with no reasoning, or visible discomfort discussing it. ₹6L sits exactly at your ceiling (₹5L cap + 20%). At ₹5L he still gets a 178% raise, so you have real room.
X2 MUST “This is a trainee role converting to full-time. Can you start now?” Available immediately, understands the trainee framing. Notice period, or expects a full PM title on day one. Your locked criterion: students and fresh grads must be able to join now as a trainee.
X3 MUST “What do you want to ask me?” Questions about the product, the data, what he'd own in month one. Specific. Only perks, title, and growth path. Or nothing. The last honest signal in any interview. What he's curious about is what he'll dig into.
