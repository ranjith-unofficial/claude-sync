Inc42 Datalabs · Product strategy · 21 Aug 2026

Winning back a cancelled mandate

The dunning system is built. What isn't built is an answer to the hardest case: a Datalabs Pro member opens GPay, kills the AutoPay mandate, and we never got a chance to say anything. This is the strategy for that moment — and for the 30 days after it.

Scope Datalabs Pro only · Razorpay recurring · UPI AutoPay Companion to the Notion system doc Author Ranjith
01 — THE READ

What this is actually about

  • The plumbing is done; the persuasion isn't. The Notion doc solves detection, classification, state, and the payment mechanics. Every open item in it is engineering. Nothing in it decides what we say, to whom, in what order, and what we offer instead. That's this document.
  • Pro is a meter, not a wall — and that decides most of what follows. Core and Pro see the same database: 75,000+ company profiles, investors, funding rounds, financials, MCA filings, cap tables, verified contacts. Pro buys export credits (0 → 50/mo, 100/mo on annual) and AI query headroom (5 → 25/day). Three consequences run through this whole document: the value we deliver is countable, the churn is predictable weeks ahead, and dropping to Free is unusually soft — they keep the product, they lose the capacity.
  • We don't own the cancel surface. The cancel happens inside GPay / PhonePe / Paytm. There is no cancel button of ours to intercept, so the entire published save-flow playbook — exit surveys, save offers, pause prompts, the 34% deflection rates — is structurally unavailable to us at the moment it matters. Everything has to move post-hoc.
  • But we get told, and we get time. token.cancelled fires in near-real-time, mid-cycle, while the person still has paid access. The gap between mandate death and service death — up to 30 days monthly, up to a year on annual — is the whole winback asset. The system already preserves it (pending-cancel + stashed next_payment). Good call.
  • A cancelled mandate is not a stated intention to leave. It is one fact: they don't want a standing instruction on their bank account. Treating it as "they want to churn" is the mistake that makes the current single-rung winback ("re-register auto-pay for ₹10") ask them to undo the exact thing they chose to do.
  • Judged as revenue, this project is small. Judged as QIA, it's the point. See §10 — this reframe is what protects it from being killed on an ROI test, and it changes several product decisions below.
The one-line strategy

Stop optimising for recovered rupees and optimise for a qualified person who stays identified and active. Then build a winback ladder where every rung asks for less trust than the one above it — and where the bottom rung is free.

02 — POSITIONING

Why the SaaS playbook gives the wrong answer here

Per the One Inc42 strategy (Utkarsh, 13 Aug 2026): membership is never the primary revenue channel — its purpose is engagement and retention; price maximises the number of qualified people in a paid relationship, not revenue per head; the north star is QIA-30. Datalabs already embodies this — the database is open on Core and Pro meters it. We are not selling access; we are selling capacity. That changes the objective function, and therefore the decisions.

DecisionSaaS defaultOur callBecause
Access during grace Hard-wall to force payment Keep full access for the whole 15-day grace. Soft in-product prompt only. A hard wall on every visit converts involuntary churn into deliberate churn, and it destroys the QIA — which is the asset the primary business sells.
Downgrade to free Fight it; it's lost MRR Offer it explicitly. Count it as a save. Core is not a crippled tier — it is the same database, minus exports and AI headroom. A member who lands on Free keeps using Datalabs daily: still identified, still active, still QIA. That is a success state, not a loss.
Discount to save Lead with it — accepted 62% of the time Last resort, and never in a utility message. Discount answers a price objection. Mandate cancellation is usually not a price objection. Also: a discount reclassifies a WhatsApp utility template as marketing.
Success metric Recovered MRR Retained QIA-30, then recovered ₹. Reporting this as a revenue project sets a bar it can't clear (§10) and invites the wrong optimisations.
Tone "Your account will be suspended" Editorial, not billing-ops. We're an intelligence product, not a vendor. Consumer sentiment in India is actively hostile to autopay right now (§03). Debt-collector tone lands on a primed audience and confirms the frame.

Two guardrails from the strategy doc. §11 lists "paid reactivation of the dormant base" as not-building — that's paid media to the dormant audience; this is owned-channel winback of paying members. Different thing, worth saying out loud before someone reads it as a conflict. §11 also rules out a Datalabs enterprise sales motion (0 renewals from 17 expired terms). So no rung in §06 routes a churning Pro member to a human or to the Team plan, however tempting that looks for a high-usage account. Self-serve or nothing.

03 — THE UPI PROBLEM

Five structural facts nobody's playbook accounts for

FactWhat it means for usWhat we do about it
The cancel happens in a third-party app. We can't intercept, can't survey, can't offer. Our save rate at the moment of cancel is structurally 0%. Every industry deflection benchmark assumes a click we never get. Move the whole flow post-hoc (§05, §06) — and build our own mandate-management surface so some cancels start with us instead (§07, F2).
NPCI forces a re-decision every cycle. A pre-debit notification is mandatory 24h before every debit, names the merchant and amount, and the same screen carries pause and cancel. We are legally required to put a cancel button in front of every member, attached to a price, with zero context about value — 12× a year on monthly, 1× on annual. Two things. Land a value message alongside the PDN (§07, F1). And push annual as an exposure argument, not a discount argument (§06, rung 3).
Our own dunning can cause the cancel. ~20M UPI mandate revocations/month in India, largely triggered by failed debits on low balances. A naive retry loop manufactures mandate deaths: debit fails → retries fire → notifications pile up → user kills the mandate to stop the noise. Cap and space retries; stop retrying once the customer has been told and given a link. Treat retry count as a churn-risk input, not a free lever (§08, G2).
The environment is primed against autopay. "Subscription traps" named as dark patterns by Consumer Affairs; RBI complaint surge; Kuku FM and STAGE publicly named; ~1 in 4 Indians report a mandate they can't explain. A real share of our cancels are mandate spring-cleaning with no opinion about Inc42 at all. Also: any aggressive recovery tactic risks landing us in that narrative. This cohort is the most winnable and needs the least persuasion — it needs recognition, not a pitch: "you cancelled Inc42 — was that deliberate?"
Pause exists, and we don't handle it. GPay and PhonePe let users pause a mandate. Razorpay fires a webhook carrying pause_initiated_by. A customer-paused mandate can only be resumed by the customer. Today a pause would fall through our classifier. Worst case: we email "re-authorize your mandate" to someone whose mandate is alive and deliberately paused. That is the single worst message we could send. Add paused as a first-class state before launch. It's a live gap, not a nice-to-have (§08, G1).
04 — THE JOURNEY

How people come in, and the four ways they leave

In

  • Search or a company profile is the front door. /company/ is the #1 surface — 1,777 users and 9,134 views per 30 days. Repeat use is driven by the workflow actions, not browsing: Advanced Filter 53%, Saved Search 52%, plain Search 44%.
  • The gate is a cap, not a paywall. Nobody hits a locked door — they hit 0 export credits left or 5 AI queries used today. So conversion happens at a specific moment of frustration, at peak need.
  • → 7-day Pro trial → ₹10 mandate registration → converts day 7 at ₹1,499 + GST = ₹1,768.82/mo.
  • Peak-need conversion means episodic demand. A member often subscribes for a thing — a fundraise, a deal screen, a pitch, a report. When the thing ends, the need ends, and the mandate dies. That is not dissatisfaction and it should never be answered with a discount. It is the strongest pause and credit-pack cohort we have (§05, §06).
  • The sharpest edge in the funnel is day 6. The member approved ₹10. The pre-debit notification 24h before conversion says ₹1,768.82 — 177× the number they consented to — with pause and cancel on the same screen. Industry: 40–60% of intro-offer subscribers churn at promo expiry vs 15–25% at full price. Almost certainly our #1 cancellation moment, and nothing in the current system addresses it.
  • And almost nobody arrives identified. Datalabs has the worst identification cliff of any Inc42 surface: 135,909 visitors / 30d, 62% activate on a company profile, 211 identified — 0.16%. The paid base is drawn from an extremely thin identified layer, which is exactly why losing one to a dead mandate is expensive in QIA terms even when it's cheap in rupees.

Out

Four exits, four different truths, four different responses. Mixing them is the failure mode the system was built to prevent — the taxonomy below is the doc's, extended with what each exit means commercially.

ExitStateWhat it actually tells usRight responseStatus
Transient failure — no balance RETRY LOOP A liquidity blip. Not churn. ~41% of all declines industry-wide are insufficient funds and they're the most recoverable class there is. Retry + pay-now link. Never mention the mandate. BUILT
Dead mandate — token unconfirmed / expired / rejected NEEDS RE-AUTH Plumbing failure. Usually our bug or the bank's, not a decision. One combined charge that pays and re-registers. Apologise for the friction, don't sell. BUILT
Mandate killed in the UPI app PENDING-CANCEL Ambiguous — and this is the whole question. Could be hygiene, sticker shock, annoyance, or genuine exit. We can't ask at the moment, so we infer (§05). Segment, then run the ladder (§06). STATE BUILT · STRATEGY GAP
Voluntary cancel on our surface PENDING-CANCEL Real intent — and the only exit where we get to ask why. Today this path barely exists because there's nowhere to do it. Intercept properly: one-question reason, ladder offered as the answers. GAP
Mandate paused in the UPI app UNHANDLED The single most positive churn signal available — 3 in 4 who pause come back. And the mandate is still alive. Recognise it, confirm it, schedule a return nudge. Never ask them to re-authorize. GAP
05 — SEGMENTATION

Reading the reason without being able to ask

The practical core of this document. Everything a save flow normally learns from an exit survey, we can infer from data we already hold at the instant token.cancelled fires — and because Pro is metered, our usage signal is a number, not a guess. Segment first, message second: a single generic winback email across all six of these will underperform badly and burn the cohort that was most winnable.

The one signal that matters most

Export credits consumed per cycle. It is the thing they are paying for, it is countable, and it moves before the cancel does. A member at 0/50 for two consecutive cycles is telling us they will cancel — weeks before the mandate dies. Every prevention feature in §07 should be aimed by this number, and it is the first field to add to the churn-risk record.

SegmentSignal (all available at webhook time)WinnableWhat to send
Mandate spring-clean Cancel not within 48h of a PDN · no failed debits · exports and logins running at their normal rate HIGH Recognition, not a pitch. "You turned off auto-pay for Inc42 — deliberate, or housekeeping?" One tap to restore. Nothing else.
Post-PDN sticker shock Cancel within 48h of a PDN · especially the trial→paid PDN HIGH Answer the number. What the last cycle actually delivered, then annual (rung 3) or a smaller commitment. Never a discount first.
Dunning fatigue ≥1 failed debit in the 14d before the cancel MEDIUM Own it. "We kept retrying and that was noisy — sorry." Then mandate-free renewal (rung 1), because they've now been burned by autopay by us.
Project ended (episodic need) Exports were heavy then stopped — e.g. 40+/50 for two cycles, then near-zero · saved searches still exist · logins continue HIGH — WRONG SHAPE, NOT WRONG PRODUCT The need was real and it ended. Pause with a return date (rung 2), or credit packs if we ever build them. Never a discount — price was never the objection.
Never activated Exports at or near 0/50 for the whole life of the subscription · few AI queries · no saved searches or alerts LOW ON PRICE They bought capacity and never used it. Don't spend money here. Route to Free (rung 4) — they keep the database they were actually using. Preserving QIA is the win; the rupees are not coming back.
Trial non-intent Cancel inside the 7-day trial · zero exports, few AI queries DON'T SPEND One message: "trial ended — the full database is still yours on Core, free." Keep the identity. Exit the sequence.

Then confirm the inference with one question, post-hoc — one. Evidence across 2M+ responses: every question beyond the first costs ~6.7% of save rate. Design it so the answer options are the ladder — picking a reason and accepting an offer become the same tap.

06 — THE OFFER

A ladder, not a single rung

Ordered by how much trust each rung asks the member to re-extend. The current system has rung 5 only — which asks someone who just deliberately removed a standing instruction to create a new standing instruction. That is the wrong first ask for most of the cohort.

Keep it — without the mandate

Asks for: one payment. No standing instruction.

Pay the next cycle as a plain one-time UPI or card payment. We remind you before each renewal; nothing is ever auto-debited. This is the highest-leverage move in the document and almost nobody in India offers it.

It answers the actual objection. A cancelled mandate says "no standing instruction on my account" — it does not say "not Inc42." Cost to us: manual renewal friction, worse retention mechanics, real ops overhead. Gain: the person stays paid and stays QIA. For a SaaS that trade is bad. For a business whose membership exists to retain qualified people, it is obviously right.

"Don't want auto-debit? Fair. Pay for next month when it's due — we'll remind you, nothing gets deducted automatically."

Pause, don't leave

Asks for: a return date. Keeps the mandate alive.

1, 2, or 3 months. Access pauses, billing resumes on a date they pick. 67% of subscribers say they'd rather pause than cancel; ~3 in 4 who pause come back; pause usage is up 337% YoY. Razorpay supports merchant-side pause and resume natively.

Critical asymmetry: a subscription paused by the customer in their UPI app can only be resumed by the customer. One paused by us at their request, we can resume. So it is strictly better for us that pauses happen on our surface — another argument for F2.

Move to annual — double the credits

Asks for: more money up front, once.

Correction worth flagging: annual is not a discount on Datalabs. The pricing page shows the same ₹1,499/mo headline — what annual buys is 100 export credits/month instead of 50. So the usual "save two months" pitch does not exist here, and any copy that implies it will be caught.

The two real arguments, and they stack: twice the capacity, and one debit a year instead of twelve — twelve regulator-forced re-decisions become one. Annual billing is documented to cut monthly churn 60–80% and involuntary churn by up to ~95%; annual subscribers renew at 3–5× the rate.

Aim it. This rung is only for members who were hitting the 50-credit cap. Offering more capacity to someone who used 3 credits is an insult dressed as an upgrade. Also lines up with the T1 ladder in the strategy (₹4,999–14,999/yr), so it does double duty as A1 evidence.

Drop to Core — you keep the database

Asks for: nothing.

Softer here than at almost any subscription product, and we should say so plainly: Core is the same 75,000+ company profiles, the same investors, funding rounds, financials, MCA filings, cap tables, contacts. You lose 50 exports a month and 20 AI queries a day. You do not lose Datalabs.

Critically: saved searches, custom lists and alerts stay alive — capped or read-only, never deleted. Those are the actions that drive repeat use (Saved Search 52%, Advanced Filter 53%), so they are what gives the account gravity and makes the return trip cheap. Deleting them on downgrade would be the single most expensive small decision in this system.

Count it as a save in reporting, because under a QIA north star it is one. This rung turns a churn event into a tier change.

Reactivate auto-pay — ₹10

Asks for: a fresh mandate.

Already built, and correct — for the accidental and hygiene cancels who never meant to switch anything off. Wrong as the only offer, and wrong as the first offer to anyone who cancelled deliberately.

A rung we could have, but I can't authorise — credit packs

Because Pro is metered, there is an offer available to Datalabs that isn't available to a normal media subscription: sell the credits without the subscription. A pack of 25 exports, valid 6 months, one payment, no mandate.

It is the purest possible answer to the two largest winnable segments — project ended and didn't want autopay — because it matches episodic demand with episodic billing. It also cannibalises Pro, and it's a pricing decision, not a retention one. Flagging it as the strongest idea in this document that isn't mine to make.

And below the ladder

Stay known. Whatever else happens, never lose the identity — newsletter, app, alerts. Identity is what the primary business (rooms, sponsors, warranted composition) actually sells. A member who stops paying but stays identified and active has cost us revenue we were never counting on. A member who goes dark has cost us the asset.

08 — GAPS

Things in the current design that will bite

GapConsequenceFix
G1 No paused state A user-initiated pause has no home in the state machine. Depending on how the token reads, it either falls through silently or gets classified as a dead mandate — and we email "re-authorize your mandate" to someone whose mandate is alive and intentionally paused. Handle the pause webhook; branch on pause_initiated_by. Self-pause → suppress all dunning, schedule a return-date nudge. Ship before launch.
G2 Retry cadence is unbounded "Let WCS retry on its own schedule" is the exact loop that drives mandate revocations nationally. Each retry generates another bank notification. Cap retries, space them, and stop once the member has been messaged with a working link. Log retry count as a churn-risk feature.
G3 Single-rung winback Only offer is a fresh mandate — the one thing the member just rejected. Predicts a low yield on the largest cohort. §06.
G4 No voluntary-cancel surface worth the name cancel-at-period-end exists as an endpoint with nothing meaningful in front of it. So 100% of cancels stay un-intercepted by default. F2 + F3. This is the difference between a 0% and a 15–30% save rate on the slice we can reach.
G5 Email-only outreach plan Phase 4 assumes Customer.io email. In India that caps recovery below 10% before a single word of copy is written. F5. Decide the WhatsApp path (Customer.io → Twilio, or direct BSP) — this is a live blocker, not a phase-2 nicety.
G6 Backfill decision is still open The doc flags proactive mark_needs_reauth vs waiting. Blasting the whole broken cohort at once tells a large group of people who currently don't know they're broken that they're broken — a share will respond by cancelling properly. Proactive, but in waves with a holdout. Start with the highest-value 10%, measure cancel rate against the untouched group, then scale. This is a cheap, real experiment (§09, D5).
G7 Open REST endpoints Already flagged in the doc, and it's the one blocker that can't be traded off — user_id-param ownership checks on endpoints that move money and mandates. Ship the auth hardening before any of this is public.

Judged purely on recovered revenue, this is a low-seven-figure project that will not survive an ROI comparison against almost anything else on the roadmap. A blocker sitting under all of this

In the 30-day Datalabs cut, revenue events (278) outnumber identified persons (211) — because payment events don't carry an email. We cannot reliably join a cancellation to a person today. That breaks the segmentation in §05, the holdouts in §09, and the QIA-retention metric below. Carrying the Auth0 distinct_id onto payment and subscription events is a prerequisite for this project, not an adjacent cleanup. It's already on the H1 instrument-spine list; this gives it a second reason to move.

Metrics to define now

  • Export credits consumed per cycle, per member — the leading indicator (§05). Nothing else on this list gives weeks of warning.
  • Mandate survival curve — % of mandates alive at cycle 1, 3, 6, 12. The category decay reference is ~85% → ~70% by month 6. This is the headline health number and we don't have it.
  • Cancel-within-72h-of-PDN rate — isolates regulator-forced churn from everything else. The single most diagnostic number in the system.
  • Winback take rate, by rung and by segment — a blended number will hide which rung is doing the work.
  • QIA-30 retention after a mandate cancel — the north-star-aligned outcome, and the only metric that credits rungs 2 and 4.
  • Recovery rate by channel and message index — expect steep decay (~2.8% / 1.9% / 1.7% by email), which is the argument for stopping at three.
  • Involuntary : voluntary split — after classification. The benchmark says ~38% of media churn is involuntary; if ours is far higher, the plumbing is still broken.

12 — OPEN

Decisions I can't make alone

  • Rung 1 — will we actually offer mandate-free renewal? It's the strongest recommendation here and it has a real ops cost. Needs Utkarsh, not me.
  • Rung 4 — does downgrade-to-Core count as a save in reporting? The QIA logic says yes, and on Datalabs it's stronger than usual because Core is the same database. Someone owns that call before the dashboard is built.
  • Credit packs — are we willing to sell capacity without a subscription? The best-fitting offer for our two most winnable segments, and a pricing decision rather than a retention one.
  • Do saved searches, custom lists and alerts survive a downgrade to Core? If they're deleted, rung 4 stops working and the return trip gets expensive. Cheap to decide now, painful to retrofit.
  • WhatsApp path — Customer.io → Twilio, or a direct BSP. Blocks F5, and F5 blocks most of the recovery upside.
  • The baseline pull. Datalabs Pro sub count, cancellation volume, mandate survival, monthly-vs-annual mix, and export-credit consumption all sit in WooCommerce / Razorpay / the warehouse, not PostHog. Every number in §10 is a benchmark standing in for data we own and haven't looked at.
