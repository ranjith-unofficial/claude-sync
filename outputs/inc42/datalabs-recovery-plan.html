03 — P0 · THE RETRY SYSTEM

47 failed charges, zero retries, ever

One failed debit is terminal 100% of the time. 111 of 113 billing cycles have exactly one attempt recorded.

Check this before building anything

WooCommerce Subscriptions ships with a failed-payment retry system that is off by default. Zero retries across 47 failures is exactly what a disabled retry system looks like. This may be a setting, not a build. Confirm before scoping engineering time.

B1 — Retry eligibility by failure code

Retrying the wrong failure is worse than not retrying: every attempt generates another bank notification and pushes the customer toward killing the mandate. 30% of our failures are dead mandates — retrying those is pure noise.

FailureShareRetry?Route
Insufficient balance45%YES — up to 3Retry schedule, then pay-now link
TOKEN_NOT_CONFIRMED30%NEVERRe-auth — mandate does not exist
Mandate inactive / cancelled10%NEVERWinback path
Bank declined5%ONCEThen re-auth
Card expired / blocked—NEVERRe-auth

The Notion classifier already makes the transient-vs-dead distinction. The retry layer only has to respect it.

B2 — The schedule

Insufficient balance is a timing problem, not a money problem. UPI AutoPay debits a bank account at one fixed instant with no second look.

AttemptWhenRationale
1Day 7, as scheduledThe original debit
2+3 daysCovers short-term cash flow
3+7 daysSecond window
4Next salary window (1st–3rd)Indian salary credits cluster at month-end and the first few days. Timing to it will outperform a fixed 48h cadence by a wide margin.
Stop4 attempts or grace deadlineWhichever comes first. Grace is 15 days.
The constraint nobody mentions

Every UPI debit needs a pre-debit notification 24 hours in advance. So a retry is decide → send PDN → wait 24h → attempt. Two consequences: the cadence can't be tighter than ~48h per attempt, and every retry puts another cancel prompt in front of the customer, because the PDN screen in GPay carries pause and cancel buttons. Nationally ~20M mandate revocations a month are driven by failed debits doing exactly this.

Action: confirm with Razorpay whether they auto-fire the PDN on retry or we trigger it. That answer sets the cadence. This is the cap-matters-more-than-count rule: three well-timed attempts with a message alongside beat six silent ones.

How to contact them

  • A person sends these, not a campaign. Real name, real reply-to, no template header.
  • The automated lane has already been tried and killed. Customer.io reactivation produced 3 logins from 1,528 visitors — 0.2% against a pre-set 0.8% kill threshold. It's been dark since 6 Jul. The one send that worked was MoEngage at a 37.8% login rate and hasn't been repeated in ten weeks.
  • Recognition, not a pitch. Most of these people didn't make a decision about Datalabs — they were in GPay doing a mandate cull, or they paused and forgot.
  • Revoked: "Your auto-pay for Datalabs is off. Access runs to {date}. Was that deliberate?" One question, real reply address.
  • Paused: nothing until their resume date, then a single nudge.
  • Write down every reply. That transcript is the segmentation, and it's the only thing that justifies building anything in P2.

09 — OPEN DECISIONS

Needs a call, not analysis

  • Who owns each line above. The table has no owner column on purpose — fill it before this circulates.
  • Who sends the list emails, under whose name. It should be a person, and it should be a person the recipient could plausibly reply to.
  • Whether the retry work is a setting or a build — B3a answers it and changes the estimate by an order of magnitude.
  • Grace period length. 15 days is set. On monthly billing that's half a cycle of free access after a failed debit. Worth a deliberate decision rather than a default.
  • How this is reported. On recovered revenue it's a small number. Framed as evidence for A9 — members renew and against A10 — the org can't run a recurring-revenue motion, it's the only instrumentation that will ever produce either.
