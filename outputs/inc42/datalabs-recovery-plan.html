Winning back Datalabs subscribers

When a Datalabs payment fails, nothing happens — no second attempt, ever. Roughly half of what we lose is recoverable, and almost none of it needs persuasion. This is the final set of things to do.

How a Datalabs subscription works

The pre-debit notification — the thing to understand

NPCI requires the customer to be notified 24 hours before every single automatic debit. Not the first one — every one. We trigger it through Razorpay; their UPI app and bank deliver it. It's mandatory. We can't switch it off, delay it, or change what it says.

GPay · 24 hours before Inc42 Media will debit ₹1,768.82 from your account on 14 Sep.

Tapping it opens the mandate screen inside their UPI app — which carries the controls to pause or revoke, authorised with their UPI PIN. So the cancel isn't literally on the notification; it's one tap away on the screen the notification opens. The practical effect is the same: the reminder and the exit arrive together.

Three things stack up badly. It's a cold number with no context — it says ₹1,768.82 and nothing about what they got for it. Cancelling feels free and paying doesn't, because access runs to the end of the period either way. And we have no presence at that moment — it happens in someone else's app, on a screen we don't control, and we don't even log when it fires.

Across India this is where roughly 20 million mandates a month get revoked.

Five other words used below

TermMeaning
MandateThe standing permission a customer gives their bank allowing us to debit automatically. It lives inside their GPay / PhonePe / bank app, not in our product. Only they can pause or cancel it. Razorpay calls it a "token" in logs — same thing.
RevokedThe customer killed the mandate in their UPI app. It's dead — a new one is needed before we can charge them again.
PausedThe customer temporarily suspended it. It's still alive. Only they can resume. Very different from revoked — and we currently treat them the same.
Detached mandateOur records say the subscription is active, but there's no working mandate behind it. No debit will ever be attempted. It just expires and nobody notices.
RetryA second attempt at a failed debit. We have never made one.

Three quarters take 26+ actions during the trial. Every failed day-7 charge is a real loss, not a tyre-kicker we're better off without. This removes a "don't spend money on low-value trials" rule that earlier drafts of this plan had — it described 3.5% of people.

There is only one plan, and it is monthly

Every self-serve subscription is ₹1,499 + 18% GST = ₹1,768.82, monthly. One price, no discounting, no annual option, no seats, no tier above Pro.

So every member receives twelve pre-debit notifications a year — twelve forced re-decisions, each with an exit one tap away. There is currently no way for a customer to choose fewer, and no way for us to offer it.

Data caveat: PostHog's Pro Membership Type still carries "Quarterly" and "Yearly" values, with 2 and 1 completions in 90 days. These are legacy or manually-created subscriptions, not purchasable plans — don't read them as evidence of an annual option that customers are ignoring.

The instrumentation can't be trusted on money

  • Payment Completed fired zero times from mid-June to 13 July, then started working. Any conversion read across that window is wrong.
  • Payment Completed_Backend on "Free Trial" shows 291 people in 90 days — that's the ₹10 authorisation, not revenue. Read as sales it is out by a factor of six.
  • Money must come from Razorpay and Metorik. Nobody should build a revenue view on these events.

We are blind where it matters most

  • Session replay, surveys and heatmaps are all switched off in the Datalabs project. We cannot watch a single person fail at checkout, and we cannot ask one churning customer why. Both are free and need no engineering.
  • We don't log when the pre-debit notification fires — so we cannot test whether cancellations cluster in the 72 hours after it. That is the single most diagnostic question in this project.
  • Nothing joins a payment to a person. Payment events carry no identity.

04 — THE SIX LEVERS

What to do, in order

W1 · Turn retries on

47 failed charges since launch. Zero retries, ever. One failed debit is terminal 100% of the time — while our own emails tell customers "we'll retry automatically."

Check first: WooCommerce Subscriptions ships with retry switched off by default. Zero retries across 47 failures is exactly what that looks like. This may be a setting, not a build.

AttemptWhenWhy
1Day 7, as scheduledThe original charge
2+3 daysCovers short-term cash flow
3+7 daysSecond window
41st–3rd of the monthIndian salaries land at month-end. Timing to payday beats a fixed schedule by a wide margin.
StopAfter 4, or at 15 daysWhichever comes first

Only retry what's worth retrying. Not-enough-balance yes, up to 3. Bank declined once. Never retry an unconfirmed, cancelled or inactive mandate — that's 40% of failures and retrying them achieves nothing while generating another notification with a cancel button on it.

Bar: ≥50% of insufficient-balance failures recover within 15 days. Baseline is 0%, so the read is unambiguous. Worth ~11 of the 21.

W2 · Catch the unconfirmed mandate in minutes, not on day 7

All 14 unconfirmed cases had their ₹10 captured five or six days before the charge failed. We had a week of warning and used none of it. The ₹10 goes through, the customer sees "trial started", and the real charge was never going to work.

Change: if the mandate isn't confirmed within minutes of the ₹10, prompt in-session, resend the request, and don't tell them the trial has started.

Bar: unconfirmed drops below 10% of failures, from 30%. Worth ~8 of the 21 — and it's a bug fix, not a campaign.

W3 · An in-product banner instead of email

Every one of our 31 payers logged in during the last 30 days. No trialist is inactive. They all come to us — we don't need to chase them.

Change: add a payment-failure value to User Type. It already carries Free / Pro Trial / Pro Member / Plus Member. One property change makes every message below targetable.

Mandate stateBannerAction
Never confirmed"One step left — approve the auto-pay request in your UPI app."Resend request
Cancelled / inactive"Auto-pay is off. Your access ends 14 Sep."Turn it back on
Payment failed"₹1,768.82 didn't go through. We'll try again on 17 Sep."Pay now
Paused"Auto-pay resumes 14 Oct."Nothing to do

Bar: ≥3% reactivation. The baseline is the 0.2% the Customer.io email lane produced before it was killed against its own 0.8% threshold. That's a 15× bar on a channel that has already failed here once.

W4 · Work the standing lists by hand

The paused count needs pulling — the system logged 263 notes, but that's a repeating check writing several notes per person, not 263 people.

A person sends these, not a system. Real name, real reply-to. A few dozen people is an afternoon's work, and the automated lane has already failed here. Opener: "Your auto-pay for Datalabs is off. Your access runs to {date}. Was that deliberate?" One question. Write down every reply — that's what tells us whether anything else is worth building.

W5 · Offer pause before they reach for cancel

Roughly three in four people who pause a subscription come back. Razorpay supports it natively. We don't handle it at all — when a paused mandate is detected the system writes a note reading "Monitoring."

Placement: on the membership page below the plan details, and as the first offer in any cancel flow. Cult.fit's model, not Netflix's — Datalabs is bought for a project, so taking a break is normal behaviour here, not an escape hatch.

Fix immediately regardless: suppress all dunning to paused customers. Telling someone to re-authorise a mandate they deliberately paused is the worst message in the system.

Bar: ≥15% of people who start a cancel choose pause instead.

W6 · Build a longer billing cycle DECISION FIRST

This is the only lever here that doesn't exist yet. There is no annual or quarterly plan to sell — monthly is the only self-serve option. So this isn't a messaging change, it's a pricing and product decision.

The argument is exposure, not revenue. A monthly member gets twelve pre-debit notifications a year. A quarterly member gets four. An annual member gets one. Every one of those notifications is a re-decision we didn't choose to run, presenting our price with no context and an exit one tap away.

The smaller version: quarterly cuts the prompts from twelve to four and asks the customer for ₹5,306 instead of ₹17,626. Materially easier to sell than annual, and it captures most of the benefit.

Sell it as fewer interruptions, not savings — whatever the price, the pitch is one debit instead of twelve.

Bar: ≥10% of new trials choose the longer cycle when offered on day 5. This shrinks the denominator rather than improving recovery — fewer chances to lose them at all.

Do W1–W5 first. They need no approvals; this needs a pricing call.

06 — DECISIONS & EXCLUSIONS

What needs a call, and what's out of scope

Four decisions

  • Who owns each task. Fill the owner column before circulating.
  • Who sends the emails, under whose name. Someone the customer could plausibly reply to.
  • Grace period. Currently 15 days of free access after a failed payment — half a billing cycle. Decide it deliberately rather than leaving it at the default.
  • Whether to build a longer billing cycle at all (W6). Monthly is the only plan today, so every member gets twelve cancel prompts a year and we have no way to offer fewer. Quarterly captures most of the benefit at a fraction of the ask.
  • How this is reported. On recovered revenue it's a small number. As the first real evidence of whether Datalabs members renew at all, it's the only instrumentation we have.

Not doing, and why

IdeaWhy not
Redesign the export-limit promptLimit Reached fired 8 times for 3 people in 60 days, all on the AI cap. Only 40 people exported anything in 30 days.
Credit packs as a win-back offerZero purchases since April; two people touched the flow in 30 days. Three of four packs have no purchasable SKU.
Annual sold as "twice the credits"Credits are not what anyone is short of — and there is no annual plan to sell. See W6.
Automated win-back campaignAlready tried, already failed at 0.2%. Revisit only once a human has proven a message works.
"Don't chase low-value trials"Removed. It described 3.5% of trialists — nobody starts a trial and does nothing.
Out of scope for this document

Acquisition and conversion work is deliberately excluded here, but two findings are large and shouldn't be lost: 513 people clicked upgrade on a "Pro Contacts" plan in 90 days and not one reached checkout, and people who use two advanced filters in week one return at 41.9% against 10.4% for those who use none, with only 11.3% ever using one.
