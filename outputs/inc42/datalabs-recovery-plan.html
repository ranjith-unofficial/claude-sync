03 — PROBLEMS & SOLUTIONS

Seven problems

Ordered by what they're worth. P1 and P2 together are 19 of the 21 recovered subscriptions.

P1

A failed payment is never tried again

47 failed charges since launch. Zero retries, ever. One failed debit is terminal 100% of the time — while our own emails tell customers "we'll retry automatically." Not enough balance is 45% of failures, and UPI debits at one fixed instant with no second look.

Solutions
  1. Check whether the retry setting is simply off. WooCommerce Subscriptions ships retry disabled by default. Zero across 47 failures is exactly what that looks like. This may be a config change, not a build.
  2. Retry only what's worth retrying. Balance and bank-declined yes. Never an unconfirmed, cancelled or inactive mandate — that's 40% of failures, and each pointless attempt fires another notification with a cancel button on it.
  3. Time it to payday. Day 7 → +3 days → +7 days → 1st–3rd of the month. Indian salaries land at month-end. Stop at 4 attempts or the 15-day grace deadline.
  4. Log every attempt — number, time, failure code, outcome. Today 111 of 113 cycles have exactly one attempt recorded, so there's nothing to tune against.
Experiment

Bar: ≥50% of insufficient-balance failures recover within 15 days.

Baseline is 0%, so the read is unambiguous. At ~10–15 failures a month this is a decision with a pre-committed rule, not a significance test. Worth ~11 of the 21.

P2

We start trials on mandates that were never confirmed

30% of first-charge failures. All 14 cases were UPI, and all 14 had their ₹10 captured five or six days before the charge failed. The ₹10 goes through, the customer sees "trial started", and the real charge was never going to work. We had a week of warning and used none of it.

Solutions
  1. Don't mark the trial active until the mandate confirms. Payment capture alone isn't proof — with UPI the ₹10 can capture while the mandate is still pending.
  2. Prompt in-session if it doesn't confirm within minutes: "One step left — approve the auto-pay request in your UPI app." They're still on the page.
  3. Resend the mandate request automatically rather than waiting for them to notice.
  4. Daily sweep for any active subscription without a confirmed mandate. 9 paying subscriptions and 5 of 25 current trials are in that state right now and will expire without a debit ever being attempted.
Experiment

Bar: unconfirmed mandates drop below 10% of failures, from 30%.

This is a bug fix, not a campaign. Worth ~8 of the 21.

P3

When a mandate dies, we have no way to tell them

73 subscriptions are revoked right now with no failed charge yet — mandate dead, charge date not yet reached, still holding full access. None contacted. And User Type, which already knows Free / Pro Trial / Pro Member / Plus Member, has no failure state at all.

A banner only reaches people who open Datalabs. We don't know how many of the broken cohort still do — 30 of 31 payers logged in last month, but that says nothing about someone whose mandate died and who has drifted. So the design has to be a cascade, not a single channel.

Solutions — in reach order
  1. Add a payment-failure value to User Type. One property change makes every message below targetable. Nothing else works without it.
  2. Day 0 — in-product banner, for anyone who opens Datalabs. Four states, one component. Free, instant, and it lands while they're using the thing they're about to lose.
  3. +48h — WhatsApp, if they haven't logged in. We already collect a phone number at checkout. Utility templates permit failed-payment and subscription notices; ₹0.115 a message. The message carries the fact and a link — no offer, or Meta reclassifies it as marketing.
  4. +5 days — email, if still nothing. Weakest channel: the Customer.io reactivation lane produced 3 logins from 1,528 people (0.2%) and was killed against its own 0.8% threshold.
  5. High value — a person writes. The 9 detached payers are ~₹15,919/month. Real name, real reply-to.
Mandate stateWhat the message saysAction

Experiment

Measure the reach split first. Of people whose mandate dies, what share opens Datalabs within 7 days? That single number decides how much weight sits on the banner versus WhatsApp — and we don't have it today.

Bars: banner ≥3% reactivation · WhatsApp ≥3% · email beats its 0.2% baseline. Cascade total ≥8%.

P4

We find out too late to do anything

Revocations surface through a job that polls Razorpay occasionally, not the webhook that could tell us instantly. In 0 of 91 cases did we record the revocation before the next charge had already bounced. The window where the customer still has access and hasn't really decided anything is gone by the time we notice.

Solutions
  1. Wire the token.cancelled webhook so cancellation is recorded the same day.
  2. Keep polling as a backstop, not as the primary path.
  3. Alert on any active subscription with no next charge date — that's the silent-expiry case from P2 and it needs a monitor, not a report.
  4. Read all three note sources in churn reporting. 93 "customer cancelled" notes sit on parent orders and 17 on renewal orders with no counterpart on the subscription record — reading subscription notes alone misses 110 cancellations.
Experiment

Bar: 100% of revocations recorded before any charge is attempted. Currently 0 of 91.

Not really a test — it's the precondition that makes P3 possible at all.

P5

A paused mandate gets treated like a dead one

The reconciliation job has logged paused tokens 263 times, with a note that reads "Monitoring." Nothing fires. A pause is the opposite of a cancellation — the mandate is alive, and roughly three in four people who pause a subscription come back.

Solutions
  1. Suppress all dunning to paused customers, today. Telling someone to re-authorise a mandate they deliberately paused is the worst message in the system.
  2. Branch on pause_initiated_by — a customer-paused mandate can only be resumed by them, so the message differs from one we paused.
  3. One nudge on the resume date. Nothing before it.
  4. Offer pause ourselves, before they reach for cancel in GPay. On the membership page below the plan details, and as the first offer in any cancel flow. Cult.fit's placement, not Netflix's — Datalabs is bought for a project, so taking a break is normal here.
Experiment

Bar: ≥15% of people who start a cancel choose pause instead.

First, pull the distinct count. 263 is note occurrences from a repeating poll, not 263 people.

P6

Twelve cancel prompts a year, and we're absent for all of them

Monthly is the only plan, so every member gets twelve pre-debit notifications a year — twelve times our price appears cold, with no context, next to an exit. We don't even log when they fire, so we can't test whether cancellations cluster around them.

Solutions
  1. Start logging the notification timestamp. Cheap, and it unlocks the single most diagnostic question in this project: do cancellations spike in the 72 hours after one?
  2. Land a message 6–12 hours before it naming what the membership actually did that cycle. We can't change their screen, but we can land beside it — turning "₹1,768.82?" into "₹1,768.82 for that."
  3. Day-5 trial message, before the day-6 notification. They approved ₹10; the alert says ₹1,768.82 — 177× the number they consented to. Sharpest edge in the funnel.
  4. Build a longer billing cycle. DECISION None exists today. Quarterly cuts twelve prompts to four and asks for ₹5,306 rather than ₹17,626 — most of the benefit, far less resistance. Sell it as fewer interruptions, not savings.
Experiment

Bar: ≥20% fewer cancellations in the 72-hour window after a notification.

If a longer cycle ships: ≥10% of new trials choose it. Baseline is zero because it doesn't exist. This shrinks the denominator — fewer chances to lose them at all.
