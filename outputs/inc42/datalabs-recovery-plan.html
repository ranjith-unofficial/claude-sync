Datalabs subscription recovery

When a Datalabs payment fails, nothing happens. No second attempt, ever. Some subscriptions expire without a single charge being tried, and dozens of customers have quietly switched off their auto-pay without anyone contacting them. This is what to fix, in order.

01 — START HERE

How a Datalabs subscription works

Worth reading even if you know Datalabs well. The problems below live in the payment plumbing, which almost nobody has had reason to look at.

Six words used throughout

TermMeaning
Mandate The standing permission a customer gives their bank allowing us to debit them automatically. It lives inside their GPay / PhonePe / bank app, not in our product. Only they can pause or cancel it. Razorpay calls it a "token" in logs — same thing.
Pre-debit notification An alert NPCI requires us to send 24 hours before every debit. It appears in the customer's UPI app naming Inc42 and the amount — with pause and cancel buttons on the same screen. Regulation forces us to prompt a re-decision every cycle.
Revoked The customer killed the mandate in their UPI app. It's dead — a new one is needed before we can charge them again.
Paused The customer temporarily suspended the mandate. It's still alive. Only they can resume it. Very different from revoked — and we currently treat them the same.
Detached mandate Our records say the subscription is active, but there's no working mandate behind it. No debit will ever be attempted. It just expires and nobody notices.
Retry A second attempt at a failed debit. We have never made one.
02 — WHAT'S BROKEN

Four problems

1 · A failed payment is final

47 recurring charges have failed since launch. Not one was ever followed by a second attempt. Nearly half of those failures were simply insufficient balance on the day — money that would very likely be there a few days later. Meanwhile our own emails tell customers "we'll retry automatically." That flow doesn't exist.

2 · Some subscriptions can never be charged at all

A subscription can lose its mandate but stay marked active. It then expires at the end of its term with no debit ever attempted. Right now 9 live subscriptions (~₹15,919/month) and 5 of the 25 current trials are in this state. They will die quietly regardless of anything else we do.

Separately, a bug lets a subscription go live on a mandate that was never confirmed — the ₹10 goes through, the customer sees "trial started", and the real charge at Day 7 was never going to work. That accounts for 30% of all first-charge failures.

3 · We find out about cancellations too late to act

We learn a mandate was cancelled by occasionally polling Razorpay, rather than being told the moment it happens. In 0 of 91 cases did we know before the next charge had already bounced. The window where the customer still has access and hasn't really decided anything is gone by the time we notice.

4 · Nobody has ever contacted the people affected

Four groups are sitting in our database right now, none of them contacted. When a paused mandate is detected, the system writes a note that literally reads "Monitoring." Nothing else happens.

What it has cost

Lifetime value lost to failed first charges: roughly ₹92,665. Total the business has earned since launch: ₹94,437.

The leak has cost approximately what Datalabs has made.

03 — THE PLAN

Fix the bucket before refilling it

The instinct is to go straight at winning back the customers who cancelled. That's the wrong order — they'd walk back into the same broken system. Repair the plumbing, then contact the people we've lost, then build only what those conversations justify.

StepWhy it comes hereMove on when
1 Stop subscriptions dying without ever being charged A retry system can't retry a charge that was never scheduled in the first place. No live subscription is sitting without a working mandate.
2 Build a system that tries a failed payment more than once This is where the money is, and it's pure engineering — no copy, no offer, no approvals. First-charge success is above 70%. It's 57.9% today.
3 Get told about cancellations immediately Without this we're always cleaning up after a failure instead of preventing one. A cancellation is recorded the same day it happens.
4 Email the four affected groups, by hand A few dozen people. One person can work through them in an afternoon and will learn more than any dashboard. Every reply is written down.
5 Build new product surfaces At 31 active subscriptions, a campaign system would be heavier than the thing it protects. —
04 — STEP 2 IN DETAIL

How the retry system should work

Check this before scoping anything

WooCommerce Subscriptions ships with a failed-payment retry system that is switched off by default. Zero retries across 47 failures is exactly what a disabled system looks like. This may be a setting, not a build — confirm first, it changes the estimate enormously.

Which failures to retry

Retrying the wrong failure is worse than not retrying. Every attempt sends another notification to the customer's bank app — and that notification carries a cancel button.

Why it failedShareRetry?Instead
Not enough money in the account45%YES · up to 3Then send a pay-now link
Mandate was never confirmed30%NEVERAsk them to set it up again
Mandate cancelled or inactive10%NEVERWin-back message
Bank declined5%ONCEThen ask them to set it up again

30% of our failures are dead mandates. Retrying those achieves nothing and annoys the customer. The existing classifier already tells the two apart — the retry layer just has to respect it.

When to retry

Insufficient balance is a timing problem, not a money problem. UPI debits the account at one fixed instant with no second look.

AttemptWhenWhy
1Day 7, as scheduledThe original charge
2+3 daysCovers short-term cash flow
3+7 daysSecond window
41st–3rd of the monthIndian salaries land at month-end. Timing a retry to payday will beat a fixed schedule by a wide margin.
StopAfter 4, or at 15 daysWhichever comes first
The constraint people miss

Every UPI debit needs a pre-debit notification 24 hours in advance. So a retry is: decide → notify → wait a day → attempt. Two consequences.

Attempts can't be closer than about 48 hours apart. And every retry puts another cancel button in front of the customer, because that notification screen carries one. Across India, roughly 20 million mandates a month are cancelled this way — people killing auto-pay to stop the failed-payment noise.

So the cap matters more than the count. Three well-timed attempts with a message alongside them will beat six silent ones.

05 — STEP 4 IN DETAIL

The four groups to contact

GroupSizeSituationWhat to do
Cancelled auto-pay, nothing broken yet 73 Mandate is dead but their next charge date hasn't arrived. They still have full access and may not remember doing it. The only group where we're ahead of the problem. Contact them before their charge date.
Paused auto-pay TBC Mandate is still alive. They chose the reversible option. Best prospects we have. Send nothing until their resume date, then one nudge. Never ask them to re-authorise.
Paying, but mandate is detached 9 ~₹15,919/month. Will expire silently. Highest value per person. Repair or contact this week.
Trials that can't convert 5 of 25 No working mandate. Will never convert. Fix it or tell them now.

The paused count needs pulling — the system logged 263 notes, but that's a repeating check writing multiple notes per person, not 263 people.

How to write to them

  • A person sends these, not a system. Real name, real reply-to address.
  • Automated reactivation has already been tried here and failed. A Customer.io send reached 1,528 people and produced 3 logins — 0.2%, against a 0.8% threshold the team had set in advance. It's been switched off since July.
  • Recognise, don't pitch. Most of these people weren't making a decision about Datalabs — they were tidying up mandates in GPay, or they paused and forgot.
  • Suggested opener: "Your auto-pay for Datalabs is off. Your access runs to {date}. Was that deliberate?" One question.
  • Write down every reply. That's what tells us whether step 5 is worth building.

07 — DECISIONS & EXCLUSIONS

What needs a call, and what we're not doing

Four decisions

  • Who owns each task. Fill the owner column before circulating.
  • Who sends the emails, under whose name. It should be someone the customer could plausibly reply to.
  • Grace period. Currently 15 days of free access after a failed payment — half a billing cycle. Worth deciding deliberately rather than leaving at the default.
  • How this gets reported. Measured purely on recovered revenue it's a small number. Measured as the first real evidence of whether Datalabs members renew at all, it's the only instrumentation we have.

Not doing, and why

IdeaWhy not
Redesign the export-limit promptIt fired 8 times for 3 people in 60 days. Nobody comes close to the export cap — the heaviest user used 42 of 100 credits, the median 2–6.
Sell credit packs to win people backZero credit purchases since April. Three of the four advertised packs have nothing to buy behind them.
Sell annual as "twice the credits"Credits aren't what anyone is short of.
Automated win-back campaignAlready tried, already failed at 0.2%. Revisit only once a human has proven a message works.
