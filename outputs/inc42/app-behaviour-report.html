Read section 02 before quoting any Explore number

The app ships a four-step guided walkthrough, and steps 2 and 3 are Explore. It drives users onto that surface before they choose anything. Every headline Explore and Watchlist figure in this report is inflated by it, and the correction is large enough to reverse the conclusion.

What survives the correction: company data is the deepest genuine engagement in the app — 75% of company-section views happen well after the tour ends, and unguided users reach it on their own.

01

The activation funnel

Onboarding is not the problem. It is one of the healthiest first-run flows you could ask for. Everything breaks at one specific door.

Onboarding loses 16% across three screens. That is excellent. People pick a role, then choose 4.4 sectors and 5.2 topics on average — they are not rushing through, they are genuinely configuring the product. There is no meaningful abandonment anywhere in the setup flow.

Then 87% reach the brief cover and only 48% walk through the door. 70 people looked at the front page of the product's core feature and left. That single step destroys more users than every other step in the app combined.

81% of people who start the tour finish all four steps. Of the 27 who bail, nearly all bail immediately — 16 of 27 dismiss it on the very first screen. Almost nobody quits mid-tour. So the tour is either accepted wholesale or rejected on sight.

Two numbers that need correcting

Cover-to-brief entry is 55%, not 41%. The 17 Aug review measured 99 → 41. Eight days on it is 156 → 86. The cliff is real but it is shallower than reported, and it is improving.

Brief completion is 38% of openers and 18% of installs. Neither figure is the "~20%" quoted in the 17 Aug Weekly App Review. Pick one denominator and publish it, because three different numbers are currently circulating for the metric you have committed to double.

The drop-off is bimodal, and that's the whole story

25 sessions — 22% — die on card one. Someone tapped into the brief, saw a single story, and left. Then almost nothing happens in the middle: cards 2 through 7 account for just 28 sessions combined. Then a huge cluster of 59 sessions reaches card 8 or beyond.

People either bounce instantly or go nearly all the way. There is no fatigue curve. That rules out "the brief is too long" as the problem — and it means the fix is at the entrance and the exit, not in the middle.

20 sessions reached card 8 and never completed

The single largest recoverable group. They are not disengaged — they opened more articles than the people who completed:

02

Explore's dominance is mostly the walkthrough

The raw numbers say Explore beats the brief 82% to 48%. That comparison does not survive contact with how users get there.

The app runs a four-step guided tour on first launch: step 1 brief, step 2 explore_articles, step 3 explore_companies, step 4 watchlist. It walks people onto Explore and Watchlist before they have chosen anything. 134 of 179 users went through steps 2 and 3.

The control groups

GroupUsersUsed ExploreOpened briefSaw watchlist
Guided through the Explore steps134100%55%96%
Skipped the tour at step 11644%63%25%
Never saw the walkthrough3813%5%0%

Every single user who was guided into Explore used Explore. All 134 of them. That is not a preference, it is the tour firing an event.

The honest control is the 16 people who dismissed the tour at step 1 and were never guided anywhere. Among them the ordering flips: 63% opened the brief, 44% went to Explore. The third group — 38 users who never saw the walkthrough at all — is not a clean control; they barely used the app at all (5% opened a brief), so they are mostly immediate bounces, not unguided explorers.

Corrected reading

Your hypothesis holds. Explore does not out-perform the brief on user intent — it out-performs on guided navigation. Strip the tour and the brief is at least competitive, and on the one clean control group it is ahead.

Worth noting the tour may actively cost brief entries: guided users opened the brief at 55%, tour-skippers at 63%. Small sample (n=16), but it points the wrong way for a tour whose first step is the brief.

Read this against the personalisation bet

Users spent real effort in onboarding picking 4.4 sectors and 5.2 topics — and then browse an unfiltered "Latest" feed and never touch a filter. Either the personalisation those selections drive is invisible to them, or they don't want a filtered view at all. That is a direct test of the sector-personalisation thesis, and right now the behaviour argues against it.

34 people — 19% of the base — generated 683 company-section views. Twenty each. Compare that to the brief, where 86 people generated 195 opens. A fifth of the user base is using this app as a funding-data terminal, and they are the most intense users in the product by a wide margin.

The ordering matters too: funding first, financials second, people third. That is a deal-research sequence, not a news-reading sequence. These look like investors, founders and operators checking a company — precisely the qualified audience the One Inc42 strategy is built to identify and monetise.

Why this matters beyond the app

The strategy positions DataLabs as depth behind a membership, and the app as a habit surface. In the data, the DataLabs-style surface is producing the depth and the habit surface is not. The app may be a better on-ramp to DataLabs than it is a brief-delivery vehicle — which is an argument about roadmap sequencing, not a bug.

04

The watchlist is the biggest dead end in the product

133Opened the tab
9Ever added anything
75%Views of an empty list
62%Views inside the tour window

74% of users open the Watchlist tab. 5% put anything in it. Of 269 watchlist views, 202 were of a completely empty list — people tapping into a blank screen, repeatedly. Only one user has ever removed an entity.

And the 74% is the most tour-manufactured number in the app: step 4 of the walkthrough is the watchlist. Guided users reach it at 96%, users who never saw the walkthrough reach it at 0%, and 62% of all watchlist views land inside the first ten minutes. Outside the tour window only 36 users ever open it, and only 15 on a later day.

The cause is structural, not motivational. The spec had onboarding run role → sectors → topics → watchlist. The shipped flow logs only role, sectors and topics — the watchlist step was never built. So every user arrives at a personalisation surface that has nothing in it and no path that ever filled it.

This is not a small feature gap. The watchlist is what makes alerts, "relevant because" reasoning, and the DataLabs on-ramp possible. All three are dormant because of one missing onboarding screen.

Fifty-seven percent of everyone who has ever installed this app used it on exactly one day. Seven people — under 4% — have built anything resembling a habit. For a product whose entire positioning is a daily habit, that is the number to carry into every roadmap conversation.

The retention engine has never been turned on

171 of 179 users were prompted for push permission. 114 granted it — 67%. And push_delivered has never fired, not once, in the app's entire history. push_opened has fired twice ever, both on 10 July.

So: a third of users come back on day one, unprompted, with the retention mechanism completely dark. The most valuable thing in this report is that the D3 cliff has never been tested against a working push.

Failures are concentrated on the two most-used surfaces in the app. 24 of 179 users saw an error screen. Explore — the thing 82% of people use — is also the thing that breaks most.

Sign-in is effectively mandatory

171 of 179 users started sign-in (96%) and 139 completed it (78%). Only 10 ever saw a contextual sign-in prompt. The spec describes an anonymous-first architecture where most users stay logged out — in production, almost nobody does. Either the flow forces it, or the option to skip is invisible.

Only 51 registered as net-new, so roughly 88 of the 139 sign-ins were existing Inc42 or Google accounts. Read carefully: this app is currently reaching Inc42's existing audience, not a new one.

09

Three things that were believed and aren't true

1. iOS 1.0.1 is live and rolling out — it is not waiting on Apple

Version 1.0.1 appeared with 3 users on 17 Aug, then 16 on 18 Aug and 27 on 19 Aug. The build shipped. Any open item saying "blocked on build ≥41" or "awaiting Apple review of the screenshots" should be closed, and the four staged screenshots checked as live.

2. Android is the platform, not iOS

111 Android users against roughly 83 on iOS. The launch-platform flip to Play Store first is being borne out by where the users actually are.

3. The anonymous-user architecture isn't what's happening

96% of installs start sign-in. The push-able-without-registration design that the spec assumes is not the lived flow, which changes how the Customer.io person-attribute machine should be wired.

The strategic question this raises

Utkarsh's 17 Aug call was to fix activation before acquisition, and the data agrees. The brief is not being out-competed by Explore on user intent — that gap was the walkthrough — so the decision to invest in brief completion stands.

What the data adds is a second, quieter question. A fifth of the base reads company funding tables eight times each, in the evening, filtering nothing, and finds those pages without being guided there. That is the most genuine engagement in the product and nothing on the roadmap serves it. Fixing the brief and serving that cohort are not in conflict — but only one of them is currently planned.

The walkthrough correction. Section 02's control groups come from walkthrough.status = 'skip_tour' at step_number = 1 (n=16) versus users reaching steps 2–3 (n=134). Sixteen people is a small control and the finding should be re-tested as the base grows — but the direction is unambiguous, because 134 of 134 guided users hitting Explore cannot be a preference. The "first 10 minutes" cut is a proxy for the tour window measured from person.created_at, not a measured tour duration.

Known limits. There is no is_internal person property, so team exclusion relies on the email property being present — internal users who never signed in are still counted. deep_link_opened is polluted with auth_callback redirects and is excluded from the deep-link reading above, which uses article_opened.source instead. decode, the AI explainer, has never fired, so nothing in this report measures it. Eight days and 188 people is directional, not conclusive.
