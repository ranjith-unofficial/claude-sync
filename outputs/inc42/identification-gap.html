What is missing, and it is material. Two of the ten qualifying actions are invisible to me: newsletter clicks (Customer.io — no access) and web article reads (scroll depth lives in GA4 and cannot be joined to identity). 2,387 of media's 3,566 registered users — 67% — have no profile. We have their email address and they have told us nothing about themselves. That is precisely why media's QIA is 55 and not several hundred.

But media has only 250 identified actives in a month. So most of cohort A is not merely incomplete — it is dormant. That makes it a win-back problem rather than a profile-completion problem, with correspondingly lower expected conversion.

The app's cohorts are almost empty, which is a good sign — its funnel is tight. Its leak is elsewhere: 322 of 969 people installed and never authenticated at all.

Enrichment is the outlier and it was not in any earlier version of this plan. It converts people who are already identified and already active into qualified ones, without asking them to do anything, without a product change, and without new traffic.

3,059 identified actives currently lack role or company. At Apollo's ~27% match rate on personal-email addresses that is 826 people, at roughly ₹2 per profile — about ₹6,000 in total. It is worth more than every product experiment on this list combined.

Why experiment 9 ranks last. "Drive more engagement" is intuitively the biggest lever and is arithmetically the smallest. Lifting media's visitor→engaged rate by a full 2.6 points adds 13,000 engaged people, who convert at 1.75%, of whom a fifth qualify. The chain multiplies down to ~40 QIA. Media's economics defeat volume interventions.

 Current pace: 34 installs on 1 September, tracking to roughly 1,000 for the month against a 5,000 target.

Between 3,000 and 5,000 installs the difference is 210–554 QIA depending on which rate holds. That range is wider than most of the experiments above, which is why the rate matters more than the volume.

Test before committing the full budget. Buy 500 paid installs and measure install→QIA on that cohort alone. If it holds above 15%, fund the rest. If it collapses below 10%, the media experiments become the plan by default. The test costs about ₹60,000 and removes the largest uncertainty in this document.

What I would actually do, in order

  1. Run enrichment this week. ₹6,000, no build, +64%. Nothing else on this list has that ratio. Legal and DPDP review first — this is third-party data on identified people.
  2. Buy 500 test installs and measure install→QIA. ₹60,000. It decides whether ₹6L of install spend is the best or worst use of September.
  3. Ship the media gate with role capture inside it. The only recurring media lever. If role is deferred to onboarding it delivers half.
  4. Run cohorts C and the app non-authenticators — nearly free, but book them as one-time, not run-rate.
  5. Drop the DataLabs gate and the engagement push from September. One needs a build for +175; the other is the worst ratio on the list.

Method. All figures are live SQL against PostHog, verified at 4 minutes' ingestion latency. Qualifying actions cover 8 of the 10 defined in the strategy: newsletter clicks (Customer.io) and web article reads (GA4 scroll, unjoinable to identity) are absent. Event applications are included from two separate event projects.

Known corruption. Media sign-ups and onboarding for May–June are a tracking break. DataLabs visitors swing 4× across May–June with no campaign behind them. The Seniority property is inconsistent across 93,000 people — mixed casing, JSON-wrapped values, four vocabularies — so qualification rates are approximate. Media has no onboarding-completion event; profile completion is proxied by having Seniority set. The app is one month of a launch cohort.

Assumptions vs measurements. Measured: cohort sizes, gate conversion (3.55%), onboarding step-1 rate (26%), install→QIA (27.7%), all funnel rates. Assumed: enrichment match rate (27%, from the 31 Aug call), and every conversion rate in experiments 4, 6, 7, 8, 9.
