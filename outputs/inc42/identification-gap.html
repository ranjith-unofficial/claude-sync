The Identification Gap

We have 490,000 people a month on one surface and cannot name 99.6% of them. We have a second surface that names people eight times better. The bridge between them is already built and 780 people used it last month.

1 September 2026 Six months: Mar–Aug 2026 PostHog 53557 · 66351 · 146258 · GA4 Inc42 v4

01 The problem, and what unification actually means

Inc42 runs three products that do not know each other. A reader who follows a company on the website starts from zero in the app and again in DataLabs. Separate logins, separate identities, no shared context.

"Unification" has been used loosely to mean design consistency. It isn't that. Utkarsh's own definition, from the 31 August call: "from a user experience, how is it consistent across all surfaces… frictionless login and onboarding." The operational version is narrower and harder: one person, recognisable on any surface.

Why it matters — the evidence, not the argument

1.6–3.4%of people use more than one surface
0.42%of media visitors are identified
2.10%of DataLabs visitors register — 5× better
780people used the 75,000 company pages last month

That last number is the whole thesis in one line. Inc42.com already hosts 75,000+ free /company/ profile pages — and they are DataLabs data, surfaced unbranded. In the last 30 days they drew 889 views from 780 people: 0.13% of site pageviews, against 426,183 people reading articles on the same domain.

The bridge between our worst-identifying surface and our best-identifying surface already exists, is fully built, and is invisible. No Follow button, no Watchlist, no link from the article about a company to the page about that company.

So unification is not a design project. It is the difference between three products each acquiring strangers separately, and one product that recognises a person and compounds.

03 How QIA helps — and exactly where it misleads

QIA decomposes into four multipliers. That is its value: every row on the plan has to name which one it moves, or it doesn't belong.

QIA = arrivals × identified × qualified × active-in-window

17,464IA-30 today (warehouse, 26 Aug)
5,470QIA-30 today
15,000QIA-30 target, FY28-end
2.7×required growth

Where it misleads

QIA-30 is binary liveness with no repeat requirement. One qualifying action in 30 days counts. Someone who read once and vanished scores identically to someone who reads daily.

The six-month data shows exactly this failure mode:

DataLabsMarAprMayJunJulAug
Registrations1,2881,6661,7762,0092,1892,417
Identified actives1,4601,7781,8441,9442,1742,160
 Registrations up 88% in six months, fully organic. Identified actives flat for two months.

QIA cannot distinguish "we acquired more strangers" from "we built a habit." It will keep rising for as long as we buy traffic, and it is currently rising while the active base stands still.

The input metric it is missing

Not a replacement — QIA is locked and Utkarsh owns it. The missing lever is:

QIA-R — the repeat rate of qualified identified actives. The share of this period's QIA who were also QIA in the previous period. Today: DataLabs 5.5%, app 29.2%, media unmeasured. Nothing on the September plan targets it, and it is the only number that separates compounding from churn.

And a decision that must come before any target

Media and DataLabs scale linearly with the window: their 30-day number is four weekly crowds of strangers added together. Confirmed on DataLabs — 94.5% of identified actives appear in exactly one week, averaging 1.16 active days a month.

Moving to QIA-7 would cut the reported number ~4× on both, and barely touch the app, with zero behaviour change. Decide the window first, set targets second.

04 What we are trying to do

One outcome, stated so it can be falsified.

Make one identified person usable across all three surfaces, so that the audience we already have compounds instead of leaking.

Explicitly not the goal this month: more traffic, more features, or a bigger app. We have 490,000 people a month. The constraint is not arrivals.

A correction to my own earlier recommendation. I claimed routing Scroll Depth to PostHog would unlock 133,000 people. It will not. QIA requires an identified person, and media identifies 1,982. Routing makes action #1 countable for those 1,982 — the other 132,000 are anonymous and score nothing.

06 September — the plan, in priority order

Ranked by information gained per rupee, not by size of the prize. The cheapest thing that could change our mind goes first.

Experiment 1 · Week 1 · O2

Is DataLabs retention even the right problem?

Hypothesis
People don't return to DataLabs because nothing brings them back — not because they have nothing to look up.
Data behind it
94.5% of identified actives appear in one week only. 1.16 active days per person per month. Only 27 people of 109,654 visitors were active in 3+ weeks.
Why it's first
The counter-case is strong and untested: DataLabs is a reference tool. You look up a company when you need one. Monthly may be the correct rhythm for the job — in which case retention is the wrong fix, coverage and SEO are the right one, and the newsletter solves a problem we never proved existed.
Method
Call 10–15 churned users. One question that separates the two worlds: did you not return because nothing reminded you, or because you had nothing to look up?
Kill criterion
If ≥60% say "nothing to look up", cancel the newsletter and move the effort to coverage and search visibility.
Cost
Five phone calls. It can cancel a built-and-waiting feature before we ship it.
Experiment 2 · Week 1 · O1

Is the 84,000 pool actually a professional audience?

Hypothesis
The people reading 3+ articles a month without signing up are the core professional market, not SEO drive-by traffic.
Data behind it
83,883 read 2+ and never identified; 37,811 read 3+. Stable across six months. But five of the top ten organic queries were once for an OTT obscenity story — a large chunk of organic traffic has historically been off-thesis.
Method
Profile the 37,811 by acquisition source, geography and content sector. No new instrumentation needed.
Kill criterion
If under 40% look professional, the acquisition case shrinks materially and O1's prize is a fraction of what this document claims.
Cost
One query. It sizes or deflates the largest number in this plan.
Experiment 3 · Weeks 2–4 · O1 + O4

Gate exposure — show the prompt to people who already read

Hypothesis
Identification is limited by exposure, not by conversion. Most engaged readers never encounter a prompt.
Data behind it
Only 11,441 of 83,883 engaged anonymous readers ever saw the registration gate — 13.5%. We have been tuning a conversion rate on a gate 86% of the audience never sees.
Method
Trigger the register prompt on the 3rd article in 30 days. Holdout group required — A2's holdout cannot be measured without Register Lock, which is now firing again (3,482 people in August, contrary to our own stale note).
Ships with
Route Scroll Depth, scroll and Plus Lock from GA4 into PostHog. Config change on tags that already fire correctly. Without it, newly identified readers still score zero on qualifying action #1.
Kill criterion
Abandon if pageviews on gated articles drop more than 8%. Articles stay open is a locked strategy constraint — gating adds identity, never price.
Target
Media page-view → signup from 0.33% to 0.6%. At current traffic that is ~1,400 additional registrations a month.
Experiment 4 · Weeks 2–4 · O3

Turn the 75,000 company pages into the bridge they already are

Hypothesis
Readers of an article about a company will follow that company if asked at the moment they are reading about it — and following is both an identification event and a return trigger.
Data behind it
75,000+ company pages exist and drew 780 people last month, 0.13% of pageviews, while 426,183 people read articles on the same domain. In-body company names are not linked. There is no Follow button anywhere on the property.
Method
Link the primary company in an article to its existing page. Put one Follow control on that page. Follow requires identity — that is the point.
Why it beats a generic gate
It asks for identity in exchange for something, at the moment of demonstrated interest, rather than interrupting a read.
Kill criterion
If company-page traffic doesn't clear 5,000 people/month by 30 Sept, the linking thesis is wrong and cross-surface hand-off needs a different mechanism.
Experiment 5 · Weeks 1–2 · App

Fix the Brief entry, and settle what activation means

Data behind it
249 people onboard and never open the Brief. 231 open it and never finish. 320 browse Explore and open nothing. Explore (653 users) is used more than the Brief (431).
Method
Land post-onboarding directly in the Brief. Ship the card-1 fix. Separately, recount activation to include an Explore article read — a decision, not a build.
The real question
A user who lives in Explore, reads three articles a week and returns weekly is currently counted as not activated. That is a measurement choice that understates the surface people actually prefer.
Why the app is not the priority
It retains 5× better than DataLabs (29.2% vs 5.5% return in 2+ weeks) and it has 907 people. It is the best mechanism at the smallest scale. Fix the funnel now; spend on installs after, not before.

07 What could go right, wrong, and what would kill this

If it goes right

Gate exposure takes media from 0.33% to 0.6% — roughly +1,400 identified people a month, more than the app produces in total. Company-page linking gives those people a reason to return that isn't a newsletter. Scroll routing means their reading finally counts. QIA rises because the base compounds, not because we bought traffic — and QIA-R, the number that proves it, is on the dashboard next to it.

If it goes wrong

FailureEarly signalResponse

The three things most likely to actually break this

  1. Owner concentration. One person is accountable for nine September rows across three surfaces. Nothing in the analysis fixes that, and it is the most probable cause of the plan not landing.
  2. The measurement jump gets banked as growth. The 30-day tracking clock clears ~20 Sept, and 7 of 10 qualifying actions are not yet piped. When they switch on, QIA rises with no behaviour change. If that is read as progress, October's plan is built on a false base.
  3. The window changes mid-flight. QIA-7 is a reasonable idea that would cut the reported number ~4× and make a working plan look like a failure.

What I could not assess

Whether newsletter clicks and event registrations — two of the ten qualifying actions, both outside PostHog — currently reach the QIA computation at all. That gap is the difference between 5,470 being the real number or another floor, and it is a half-day question for Prapti.

Four layers. The unusual one is the canary — media registrations read zero for the whole of June and nobody noticed for two months.

A daily dashboard that only watches numbers move will miss the June failure again. It has to alert on an event going to zero.

One view across three platforms

PostHog structurally cannot do this — three projects, no cross-project query. Three paths, and they are not alternatives but a sequence:

How to read these numbers. Measured in PostHog unless marked GA4, so they are floors: the official IA-30 of 17,464 and QIA-30 of 5,470 come from the warehouse, which also counts newsletter clicks and event registrations PostHog never receives.

The three PostHog projects cannot be joined, so cross-surface totals double-count. Media registrations for May and June are a tracking break, not a decline. DataLabs visitors swing 4× across May–June with no campaign behind them and are unreconciled. The Seniority property is inconsistent across 93,000 people — mixed casing, JSON-wrapped values, four vocabularies — so any qualification rate computed from PostHog alone is approximate. The app is one month of a launch cohort; its retention advantage is encouraging, not proven. Re-read after ~20 Sept when the 30-day tracking clock clears.
