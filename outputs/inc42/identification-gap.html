The Identification Gap

About 98,000 people engage with Inc42 every month and never tell us who they are. That single number explains the north star better than any other in this document.

01 What we are trying to do

The north star is QIA-30. Every plan row should end up moving one of its four multipliers, and the point of this document is to say which one is worth moving first.

IA-30 — a person we can name who took at least one qualifying action in 30 days. Two tests: we know who they are, and they did something on the list.

QIA-30 — an IA-30 person who is also the right kind of person: Senior Manager or above, at an organisation we know, and not a student, academic or service provider.

QIA = arrivals × identified × qualified × active-in-window

03 The recency structure — and what it proves

The 7 / 15 / 30-day cut is the most diagnostic thing in this document. If people return, a longer window adds fewer new names. If they do not, it adds them in a straight line.

Media481032044.25×Linear — nobody returns

Media and DataLabs scale almost perfectly with the window. Their 30-day number is not a base of returning users — it is four weekly crowds of strangers added together. Confirmed directly on DataLabs:

94.5%DataLabs actives present in one week only (1,996 of 2,112)
1.16average active days per person per month
27people active in 3+ weeks — of 109,654 visitors
29.2%App users active in 2+ weeks

The app retains five times better than DataLabs. Its problem is size, not behaviour. DataLabs' problem is behaviour, not size. These are different problems and they need different rows on the plan.

Decide the window before setting any target. Because there is no repeat, moving from QIA-30 to QIA-7 would cut the reported number by roughly 4× on Media and DataLabs and barely touch the app — with zero change in behaviour. QIA-7 was floated on 21 Aug for faster feedback loops. It is a reasonable idea that would make the plan look like a catastrophe.

Explore (653) is used more than the Brief (431). The feature we call the core product, and measure activation on, is the second most-used surface in the app. Reading is genuinely happening — 206 people opened two or more articles, a higher share (22.7%) than media manages (18.0%).

Activation is defined as brief completion, so a user who lives in Explore, reads three articles a week and returns every week counts as not activated. That is a measurement choice, and it is currently understating the surface people actually prefer.

The metric nobody currently owns

Every target on the plan is a volume target. The number that decides whether volume compounds is the repeat rate of identified actives — today 5.5% on DataLabs, 29.2% on the app. Nothing on the September sheet targets it. It should be the benchmark we set, and everything else backtracks from it.

How to read these numbers. Everything here is measured in PostHog unless marked GA4, so it is a floor: the official IA-30 of 17,464 and QIA-30 of 5,470 come from the warehouse, which also counts newsletter clicks and event registrations that PostHog never receives.

The three PostHog projects cannot be joined, so totals across surfaces overstate — the same person on media and DataLabs is counted twice. Media registrations for May and June are a tracking break. DataLabs visitors for May and June swing 4× with no campaign behind them and are unreconciled. The Seniority property is inconsistent across 93,000 people — mixed casing, JSON-wrapped values and four vocabularies — so any qualification rate computed from PostHog alone is approximate. The app is one month of a launch cohort; its retention advantage is encouraging, not yet proven.
