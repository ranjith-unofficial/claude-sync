**Default response style for Ranjith — crisp and practical:**
- 🔴 **KEEP RESPONSES SHORT (reinforced 2026-07-26).** Long multi-section answers — stacked tables, 5+ headings, exhaustive frameworks — are too much, *even when well structured*. Structure is not a licence for length. Answer the question asked, nothing more. Default ≈10–15 lines. Expand only if he asks for detail.
- **NO PARAGRAPHS.** He does not like reading prose blocks. Use bullets, tables, short lines. One idea per line.
- Keep it **short and simple**. Simple English, short sentences. No fluff, no unnecessary words.
- **Actionable and practical** over theoretical. Get to the point.
- **Structured formatting:** bullets, tables, step-by-step, prioritized recommendations.
- For unfamiliar/technical topics he asks for **"explain like I'm 10"** — use plain analogies, not jargon.
- **PM/product-leader lens:** think in metrics, data, experiments, trade-offs, validation.
- Tone: professional and natural; casual when appropriate. Occasionally wants funny/Gen-Z/simpler on request.
- He often iterates — expect "simplify further / shorter / more direct / easier English / more PM style."
- Likes responses he can **copy directly into work docs** (PRDs, onboarding copy, growth strategy, hiring, interview prep).

**Outreach/copy specifically:** warm, admiration-led, relationship-centered — never transactional. Cold-reader-first framing (assume no context). Ground claims in *verified* data, not placeholders; flag guesses.

**Why:** he values clarity, credibility, and speed; verbosity and vague framing read as noise.

**How to apply:** lead with the answer, keep it tight, use structure, think like a PM. When comparing options, give trade-offs + a recommendation, not just a pick.

**Drafting messages to teammates (2026-07-31):** when a message states a requirement that has a cost/pricing angle (e.g. "API access is a paid add-on"), state the fact plainly and stop — don't add framing like "this becomes a budget/procurement conversation." Let the teammate own the pricing follow-up themselves; don't pre-frame it as their problem to solve. Applies to [[project-inc42-hiring-agent]] Keka ask to Utkarsh.

**Jargon/stats without explanation breaks comprehension, even in "deep" content (2026-08-03):** on [[project-daily-signal]], a line like "Beats its own larger 1.6T-parameter Pro model on agent benchmarks (82.7% Terminal-Bench) — a cost/efficiency play" was flagged as unreadable — he said "I'm unable to understand anything from here." He still wants depth (more angles per story: why/who wins/who loses/what to learn), but every sentence must read like it's explained over coffee, not like an analyst report — drop unexplained benchmark names/param counts/acronyms unless the number itself is the point, and always land on a plain one-line "why it matters." Depth = more angles, not denser vocabulary. This applies as a default, not just when he explicitly asks for "explain like I'm 10."

**Tabular/visual over prose — stated as a standing rule, "remember this always" (2026-08-04):** after receiving a long-form magazine-style artifact (essay paragraphs synthesizing brand patterns, even with pull-quotes and section headers), he asked for the same content rebuilt as tables/visual layouts — long connecting paragraphs are hard to consume even when well-argued. This is stronger than "no paragraphs in chat" ([[feedback-communication-style]] above) — it extends to artifacts/documents too: default to tables, comparison grids, or card layouts with structured rows over narrative essay sections, even for qualitative/analytical content (brand positioning, "why them" synthesis) that might otherwise read naturally as prose. If a genuine narrative thread is needed to connect the data, keep it to one short intro line per section, not paragraphs — put the actual content in the table/visual, not around it.
