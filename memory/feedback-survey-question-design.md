When designing a user-research or screener survey (WhatsApp form, in-app feedback form, etc.), Ranjith wants the design grounded in actual UX-research convention and real product behaviour data — not generic/invented questions. Established through several corrections while redesigning the Inc42 App feedback form (28 Aug 2026, see [[project-inc42-app-feedback-survey]]):

- **Understand the actual job before writing questions.** First establish who the respondent is, what we're trying to learn/do, and whether the current questions serve that — only then judge or rewrite individual questions. Jumping straight to "is this question good" without that framing was explicitly rejected.
- **Funnel order**: put the most disqualifying/gating question first (e.g. "do you actually use this product"), then context, then the real ask, then logistics — not the reverse.
- **Unaided before aided**: to test whether users understand a concept/feature, ask an open, unprompted question first ("what do you do when you open the app") before ever naming the feature by name. Naming it first contaminates whether recognition is genuine.
- **Functional recall over self-assessed awareness — the strongest single correction.** Never ask "do you know what X is?" — people default to a face-saving "yes." Instead ask for a concrete fact only a real user could produce (e.g. "what's your current streak?" with "0" and "I don't know what a streak is" as valid options) — the inability to answer IS the signal, more reliable than any self-report. Also don't over-demand recall that's unrealistic even for engaged users (e.g. exact story count in a daily-changing feed) — the functional question still has to be something people could plausibly know.
- **Don't invent options that weren't discussed.** Corrected sharply after adding a "₹500 Amazon voucher" alternative-incentive option directly into a "final" form when it was only ever raised as something *to consider*, never approved. Present alternatives as flagged open questions, never bake them into a "final" deliverable as if decided.
- **State reward/incentive after establishing genuine context, not as the opening hook** — reduces false-positive interest from people answering just for the reward.
- **When told "how do other brands do this," actually research it** (web search for the specific convention — screener surveys, aided/unaided awareness testing) rather than asserting from general knowledge. Ranjith pushed back hard ("random stuff") when the design wasn't grounded this way.

**How to apply**: reuse this checklist for any future research-recruitment or feedback-form design task, for Ranjith or any team member's request routed through him.
