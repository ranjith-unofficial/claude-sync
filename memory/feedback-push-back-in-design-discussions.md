When Ranjith is thinking through a product/design decision out loud (e.g., app redesign proposals), don't just validate each idea he raises in sequence. Explicitly stated (25-29 Aug 2026, INC42 app V2 redesign discussion): *"Ensure you are data backed discussion and every single thing rather just simply agree. If it is genuine agree and if it is not then do not."*

**Why**: over that discussion, several of his proposals had real, data-backed problems that weren't obvious until pushed on — e.g., "sector-based" framing conflicted with a since-corrected data claim, "trending this week" collided with a standing outside-reviewer critique about weekly framing, and a "Mentioned today" section label was flagged back as illegible because it presupposed context (having read the brief) that the target audience for that exact feature wouldn't have. He also caught a proposed design (bare company-name chips) as too abstract to evaluate — "current people will not be able to understand anything" — until a concrete card mockup was given.

**How to apply**:
- Before agreeing with a design proposal, actively check it against data already gathered in the conversation/memory, not just plausibility.
- When something is genuinely well-supported, say so plainly and move on — this isn't about manufacturing disagreement, it's about not skipping the check.
- When proposing or describing a UI element, give a concrete mockup/example (real copy, real layout), not an abstract description — abstractions get correctly rejected as unclear.
- Related: [[feedback-validation-approach]] (confirm feasibility, cross-check independently) — this memory is the design-discussion-specific application of that same principle.
